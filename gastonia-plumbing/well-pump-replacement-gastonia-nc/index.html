    Solar Well Pump Systems in Gastonia, NC

    Solar well pumps make sense for livestock tanks and cabins where running power from the meter would cost more than the pump itself. A few panels feed a DC pump through a controller while the sun is up. The storage tank does the job a battery would. We size panels to your daily gallons.

    Well Pump Sizing in Gastonia

    Sizing starts with the well log. The log, or a sounder down the casing, gives us static water level and well depth, and a draw-down test tells us roughly how many gallons per minute the well can actually give. Pumps bigger than the well can supply run dry.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Gastonia

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Most fail from sand chewing up the impellers or a lightning surge that cooks the windings. Before pulling, we test the windings with a megohmmeter. That tells us whether the problem is down the hole or up top.

    What a Well Pump Job Costs in Gastonia, NC

    A well pump job usually runs a few thousand dollars, and the pump itself is often less than half of that. Labor and hoist time, along with new wire and pipe, make up the rest. Every line is on the quote.

    Jet Pump Seal and Motor Replacement in Gastonia, NC

    Single-stage and two-stage jet pumps wear out at the seal and impeller, and a leaking shaft seal shows up as a puddle under the motor. We replace the pump and prime it properly. Then we watch the gauge through a full cycle.

    Constant Pressure Pump Systems in Gastonia

    Franklin SubDrive and Grundfos SQE are the two constant pressure systems we install most. Both use a small 2 to 20 gallon tank instead of a big one, since the drive keeps the pump running at a matched speed. Soft starts also cut wear on the motor. The upfront cost is higher.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Replacement in Gastonia

    Plan on $1,500 to $3,500 for a typical residential job. A deeper well or a bigger pump pushes it higher, and a new pressure tank or wire run adds to it, sometimes well past that range. We quote after testing.

    A typical swap fills most of a working day. Deeper wells take longer because every 20-foot length of pipe has to come up, be unthreaded or unclamped, and go back down. We will give you a time estimate up front.

    Pulling a pump takes heavy equipment and electrical know-how. Mistakes can damage the well or hurt someone.

    Shallow well pumps, usually jet pumps, sit above ground and pull water by suction from about 25 feet or less. Deep well pumps are submersibles that hang in the water and push it up. Suction cannot lift much past 25 feet.

    Pump manufacturers usually warranty their units for 1 to 5 years against defects. Some brands extend that coverage when a licensed contractor does the installation, which is one more reason not to buy a pump online and set it yourself. Our labor warranty is on top of that.

    One to five years from the manufacturer, typically. We also provide a labor warranty.

    Sputtering faucets and dropping pressure are early warnings. A pump that runs constantly, a jump in the electric bill or water that suddenly turns cloudy can all mean the pump is wearing out. Do not wait for zero pressure.

    Location is the difference. Jet pumps stay on the surface and suck water up from shallow wells, and submersibles sit in the well and push water up from far deeper. At depth, a submersible wins on efficiency.
