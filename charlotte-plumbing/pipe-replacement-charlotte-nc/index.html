    What Goes Into a Repipe in Charlotte, NC

    PEX Pipe Replacement in Charlotte

    PEX bends around corners and comes in long rolls, so a typical repipe needs fewer fittings than copper would on the same layout. Sudden cold snaps can catch North Carolina homeowners off guard, especially in homes with uninsulated crawlspace plumbing. PEX can stretch a little in a freeze instead of splitting. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings where possible, which give a full-bore connection and hold up well over time. Faster install.

    Slab Leak Pipe Replacement in Charlotte

    Rerouting is often better than digging. Jackhammering floors is a last resort. We run new pipe through the attic or walls to bypass the leaking line under the concrete, and usually costs less than repairing in place. Fast too.

    Polybutylene Pipe Replacement in Charlotte

    Polybutylene is gray or blue plastic installed from roughly the late 1970s to mid 1990s. Chlorine breaks it down over time. Failures tend to be sudden splits at fittings rather than slow drips, and some insurers refuse to cover homes that still have it. Replace it.

    Slab Leak Pipe Repair and Replacement in Charlotte

    Water under a slab shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a sound of running water, or a meter that spins with everything off. Finding the exact spot comes first. Then you have a choice between breaking the slab to fix that spot or rerouting the line through walls or the attic, and rerouting often costs less and avoids future slab leaks on that line. We explain both.

    Corroded Pipe Replacement in Charlotte, NC

    Corrosion shows up as green or white crust on copper, rust on steel, or pinholes that drip. Water chemistry plays a role. We replace corroded sections or the whole system depending on how widespread the damage is, and we check the water heater connections and shutoffs since they often corrode too. Look at the joints.

    Emergency Pipe Replacement in Charlotte, NC

    A burst pipe needs fast action. Shut off the main valve first. Then call, because water spreading through walls and floors does damage every minute, and we can usually get there, stop the leak and replace the failed section the same day. Drying comes next.

    Homes from the 1950s and 1960s often still have original galvanized pipe. It is near the end. A full repipe brings new PEX or copper to every fixture, improves pressure, clears up discolored water, and removes the leak risk from aging threads. Worth it.
