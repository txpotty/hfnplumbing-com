    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer in Charlotte

    Annual residential testing runs $75 to $150 per assembly. A rebuild usually costs $150 to $450. New residential installs range from $300 to $1,200, and commercial RPZ assemblies often cost $1,500 to $5,000 installed once the drain and enclosure are included.

    An RPZ has two checks with a relief valve between them, and it is rated for high-hazard lines. A DCVA has two checks and no relief valve, so it is used for lower hazards like many fire lines and some irrigation systems. Code decides which one.

    We service reduced pressure zone assemblies, double check valve assemblies, pressure vacuum breakers and atmospheric vacuum breakers. Watts, Wilkins, Febco and Conbraco are the most common, and we also see Apollo and Zurn on commercial work. Common kits ride along.

    Yearly testing is the norm for water utilities. Rubber wear and grit on the seats cannot be seen from outside, so the yearly test is the only way to catch them. We send reminders.

    Watts, Febco, Zurn Wilkins, Apollo and other common brands. Kits for the common models are on board. Less common parts can usually be ordered within a few business days, and we schedule the rebuild as soon as they arrive.

    Repairs and installs from HFN Plumbing carry a workmanship warranty. We use factory rebuild kits and listed parts, and every job is retested before we leave so you start the warranty with a passing assembly. You get the terms on paper.

    Once a year is the usual requirement. Some providers require it after installation, repair or relocation as well. The deadline is on your notice, and we can usually get there within days.

    Water dripping or running from the relief valve on an RPZ is the clearest sign. A pressure drop, a split bonnet after cold weather or water with an odd taste can also mean trouble. Get it tested soon.
