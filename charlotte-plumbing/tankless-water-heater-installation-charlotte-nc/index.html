    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Charlotte, NC

    Calcium scale is the most common reason tankless heaters fail. Municipal water in North Carolina is generally soft, but many private wells produce acidic water that can corrode copper pipe over time. Scale coats the heat exchanger, cuts efficiency and triggers error codes, and left alone it can crack the exchanger and end the warranty claim before it starts. Flush it yearly. We install isolation valves for that.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Installation in Charlotte, NC

    Rinnai has been making tankless heaters for decades, and replacement parts are widely stocked. Their condensing and non-condensing lines both fit different homes, depending on the vent route and the budget you are working with. The non-condensing ones need stainless vent pipe. Condensing models use PVC vent and a condensate drain.

    Residential Tankless Water Heater Installation in Charlotte

    Sizing a home unit comes down to two numbers: peak gallons per minute and temperature rise. A shower head flows about 2 gallons per minute and a kitchen faucet a bit less, so a house that runs two showers and a sink together needs roughly 5 to 6 gallons at the rise your inlet water calls for. That narrows the model list fast.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Charlotte

    Propane homes in Charlotte are good candidates for tankless, using a model built or converted for propane. Since propane runs at different pressure from natural gas, the regulator, line sizing and gas valve must all match, and the supplier may need to swap the tank regulator. A small tank can run short.

    Tankless Water Heater Relocation in Charlotte

    Moving the heater is common on a tankless job because the old tank spot is not always the best place.
