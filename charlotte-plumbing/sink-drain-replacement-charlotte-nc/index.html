    Code Updates During Drain Replacement in Charlotte

    Old drains sometimes break current rules. S-traps and drum traps were common decades ago, and when we replace the drain we bring it up to a vented P-trap with the right trap arm length. That can mean an air admittance valve. Local rules decide.

    Vessel and Grid Drain Replacement in Charlotte

    Vessel sinks use a different drain. Most glass and stone vessel bowls have no overflow hole, so they need an unslotted grid or umbrella drain, and a slotted drain in one of those bowls leaks out of the slots right into the counter hole. We see the wrong drain often. Swapping it takes under an hour.

    P-Trap Replacement in Charlotte

    Thin chrome traps are the ones we replace most. They rust from the inside, the plating flakes off, and a slip nut that will not loosen often twists the whole trap apart, so we cut it out and install heavy-wall PVC or polished brass that matches the room. The new trap gets a full flow test. Total time is often under an hour.

    Utility and Basement Sink Drain Replacement in Charlotte

    Basement sinks often drain to a pump. If your laundry or utility sink sits below the sewer line, its drain runs to a sink pump or a sewage ejector, and we check the check valve and the discharge line when we replace the trap. Pumps fail quietly. A gurgling pit is a clue.
