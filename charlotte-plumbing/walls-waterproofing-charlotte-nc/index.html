    Wall Waterproofing Work We Do in Charlotte, NC

    Plumbing Leak Behind Walls in Charlotte

    A drip from a shower valve cartridge, a sweated copper stub or a loose drain slip nut can soak a wall cavity for months before paint bubbles. We find it with a moisture meter, a thermal camera that shows the cool wet spot, and a pressure test on the supply lines. Then we cut a clean access hole. The bad fitting or section of pipe gets replaced. Wet insulation comes out, and fans dry the cavity before we close it up.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Charlotte

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and usually add a dehumidifier to hold humidity under 60 percent. High humidity can rust exposed metal pipe and fittings under homes, so crawlspace plumbing deserves a regular look. Wood rot slows to a stop. Drier air under the floor also cuts down on the musty smell in the house.

    Basement Wall Sealing in Charlotte, NC

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. High humidity can corrode exposed metal pipe and fittings under homes, so crawlspace plumbing deserves a regular look. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then seal the face with a masonry waterproofer that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. The musty smell usually fades within weeks. The weep holes empty into interior drain tile when there is one.

    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Charlotte, NC

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Homes built from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s may still have polybutylene pipe, which is prone to sudden failures. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. A rigid coating on soft brick traps moisture and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with interior drain tile. We repoint failed mortar joints before sealing.

    Drainage System Integration in Charlotte, NC

    Water has to go somewhere once a wall is sealed. Footing drains, interior drain tile and French drains in the yard all do the same job at different depths. We match the drain to where the water comes from, which might be a high water table, a yard that slopes toward the house, or a gutter dumping at the corner. We extend downspouts at least four feet from the wall.

    Exterior Block Wall Waterproofing in Charlotte, NC

    White crust on block or stem walls is efflorescence, salt left behind as water moves through the masonry and dries. The usual cause is a sprinkler head aimed at the house instead of the bed, or soil graded toward the foundation. We fix the water first. Then we clean the wall, let it dry and apply a breathable masonry sealer or elastomeric coating.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Charlotte

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most dependable way to stop a basement leak, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing so water has somewhere to go. Coastal areas have sandy soil and high water tables, which affect how sewer lines and septic systems function. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Cost-Effective Waterproofing Solutions in Charlotte, NC

    Not every wet wall needs a $15,000 excavation. Sometimes the fix is a downspout extension and regrading the soil so it slopes away from the house, a drop of about six inches in the first ten feet, and that costs a few hundred dollars. We start with the cheapest fix that will actually work. You get the options in writing, side by side.

    Damp proofing is usually a thin spray-on asphalt coat. It slows soil moisture but cannot hold back water under hydrostatic pressure. Waterproofing uses thicker membranes, drainage boards and footing drains rated to stop liquid water, which is what a basement in wet soil or a high water table needs.

    Look low on the wall first. Damp patches, a musty smell, mold on the bottom of the drywall, white powdery efflorescence on block or brick, and puddles after rain all point to water coming through. Bowing walls and stair-step cracks need a closer look too.

    Bubbling paint is a big one. Soft drywall near the floor, stained baseboards, a musty smell and white crust on block or stucco all point to moisture, and in a shower, loose tiles or cracked grout tend to mean water has already gotten behind the wall. A moisture meter confirms it in minutes.
