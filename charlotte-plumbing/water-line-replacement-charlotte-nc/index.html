    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in Charlotte

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. Inside, the pipe rusts once the zinc is gone. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, which is why pressure drops and the water runs orange after a shutoff, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Charlotte, NC

    Bursting fits old cast iron or galvanized lines that will shatter under the head. Copper and poly are better pulled than burst. Either way the new pipe follows the old path, which matters when the route runs under a patio or a neighbor's fence, leaving you with two small holes instead of a long trench. Rock can stop it.

    Water Line Materials We Install in Charlotte, NC

    Our trucks carry Type K copper, PEX-a and HDPE. Each has a place. Copper suits short runs and high pressure, PEX handles long runs with bends, and HDPE is the usual pick for trenchless pulls and bores, where we need a tough, fused pipe that can be dragged a long way. All of it is listed for drinking water, meeting NSF 61.

    Main Water Line Replacement in Charlotte

    Your service line runs from the meter or curb stop to the main shutoff inside the house, usually as 3/4-inch or 1-inch pipe. After a second leak in a new spot, we stop recommending patches. We dig at both ends, pull or cut out the old pipe, and run new line at the depth local code calls for, with fresh connections at the meter and at the shutoff. Most homes are done in a day.

    Commercial Water Line Services in Charlotte, NC

    Commercial services run bigger, often 1-1/2 to 2 inches, and almost always have a backflow assembly that needs testing after any work. We schedule the shutdown around your hours. For a restaurant or an office building that might mean an early morning start, temporary water to the restrooms, and coordination with the water utility on the meter side. Tenants get notice.

    Damage Assessment Before Replacement in Charlotte

    We start at the meter. With every tap closed, the leak indicator should sit still. From there we listen along the line with an acoustic ground mic, check the house side for a toilet or softener running, and sometimes push a camera into the line when we suspect root damage or a crushed section, so the hole goes in the right spot.

    Copper Water Line Installation in Charlotte

    Type K copper is the thick-wall grade made for burial. Street pressure does not bother it. We bed it in sand, keep it away from sharp rock, and sleeve it where it passes through a foundation wall, because concrete and copper rubbing together for twenty years eventually wear a hole. It costs more than PEX.

Sod goes back on top.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Replacement in Charlotte

    For a typical house, figure on $2,500 to $8,000. Length and method move the number the most, because a trenchless pull under a driveway costs more per foot than an open trench across a lawn but spares you a concrete bill. Copper adds a bit. You get a written number.

    Repair fixes one spot. A short piece of new pipe goes in where the leak was. Replacement swaps the entire run from meter to house, which is the better choice once the old pipe is corroded everywhere or has failed more than once, since the next leak is usually a few feet away.

    Mountain areas see much colder winters than the coast, so plumbing needs differ quite a bit from one end of the state to the other. Hard freezes are uncommon along the coast but happen most winters in the mountains, where pipes in crawlspaces and outside walls need protection. Burying the line deep enough is what keeps it from freezing. We set new service lines at or below the frost depth local code requires, insulate the riser where it comes up to the meter, and use pipe that tolerates the swings, which in cold country often means PEX.

    Most policies cover sudden water damage but not a line that wore out. A service line rider may cover the replacement itself. Check with your agent before the work starts, and we will provide paperwork if you file.

    Homes built before about 1950 sometimes have lead service lines. Take a key to the pipe where it enters the house. If it is soft, dull gray and turns shiny where scratched, it may be lead, and it should come out. Many utilities now offer help with the street side.
