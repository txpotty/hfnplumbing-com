    Underground Water Line Work in Charlotte, NC

    Main Water Line Repair in Charlotte

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. A moving dial with the house valve closed means an underground leak. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, so the crew opens one small pit rather than trenching the whole run.

    Water Pressure Restoration in Charlotte

    Low pressure at every fixture points to the service line or the regulator, not the faucets. First step is a pressure gauge on the outside spigot. Then we check the pressure reducing valve, the main shutoff and the curb valve for anything half closed, and look at the pipe itself. Galvanized pipe can lose half its opening to rust scale. The fix might be a new valve or a new line.

    Emergency Water Line Repair in Charlotte, NC

    First thing on a burst line is shutting the water off, at the house valve if it works and at the curb stop if it does not. Free-running water washes soil out from under walks and slabs fast. HFN Plumbing sends a plumber with a curb key, a pump and repair couplings. Most breaks are shut down quickly. If the repair will run long, we rig a temporary bypass so the house has water while we work.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Charlotte

    Underground leaks hide well. Many people find out from the bill before they see anything in the yard. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or ground that stays soft long after the sprinklers shut off. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. The dig follows the sound.

    Main Water Line Replacement in Charlotte, NC

    Replacement beats repair once a line has leaked two or three times, or when it is galvanized or polybutylene. Patching that kind of pipe tends to buy months, not years. We run new Type K copper or PEX from the meter to the house, typically finishing in one day whether we trench or pull the pipe. We pull the permit, set up the inspection and pressure test the new line before it is covered.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Charlotte

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. Straight runs are ideal. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, and a length of HDPE trails behind it in the same path. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Charlotte

    A trench through a paver driveway or a mature garden can cost more to restore than the pipe itself. That is where trenchless pays off. We dig a pit at each end and pull HDPE or PEX through with a boring tool or pipe puller, following the old line or cutting a new path beside it. Most replacements finish in a day. You also get the manufacturer warranty on the new pipe.

    Underground Leak Detection in Charlotte, NC

    A ground microphone picks up the hiss of water escaping a pressurized pipe, even through a couple of feet of dirt. Quiet leaks are harder. For those we push a hydrogen and nitrogen tracer gas into the drained line and sniff for it at the surface, a method that catches leaks too quiet for acoustic gear. Coastal areas have sandy soil and high water tables, which affect how sewer lines and septic systems perform. You get a written price as soon as the leak is marked on the ground.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Charlotte

    A minor leak repair often starts around $500. Full replacement costs more. A meter-to-house replacement or a trenchless pull can top $5,000 when the run is long, deep or under hardscape, and the price moves with pipe type, depth and whether we trench or bore. We quote it in writing after the inspection.

    Two to four hours for most spot repairs. A full replacement is typically a one-day job. Trenchless jobs are often a single day too, even with a few hours spent setting the pits and the rig. In most cases your water is back on before evening.

    Age is the biggest cause. Old pipe corrodes. Shifting soil, tree roots, high water pressure and freeze-thaw cycles all add stress, and an old line gets damaged by excavation work more often than most people realize. Poor original installation is another, like rocks dumped right on the pipe.

    Watch the bill first. An unexplained spike usually comes before anything you can see. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, a wet spot in the yard that stays wet in dry weather, cloudy or rusty water and the sound of running water with every fixture off. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    For PEX we use Uponor and Viega, depending on the job. Copper is Type K for buried runs. Couplings and transition fittings come from makers like Mueller and Ford, chosen for the ground and water conditions on your lot. We do not use cheap knockoff fittings.

    Often the pipe itself is not covered. Damage it causes may be. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their insurer or the water provider, which can pay for the repair or replacement of the buried line itself. We provide photos and an itemized invoice for your claim.

    Spot repairs usually take a few hours. Plan on a day for a full replacement. Jobs with a deep line, a lot of hardscape or a trenchless pull can stretch to two or three days, mostly because of restoration work and inspection timing. We give you a time estimate with the quote.

    In most places the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. You own the rest. Some providers draw the line at the curb stop instead, so before quoting we check exactly where the leak is. If it turns out to be theirs, we tell you and help you report it.
