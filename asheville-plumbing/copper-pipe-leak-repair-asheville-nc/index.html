    Copper Pinhole Leak Repair in Asheville

    Pinholes form when the inner wall of copper pipe erodes or pits. Hot water speeds it up. They often appear in thin Type M pipe and on hot water lines where velocity and temperature are higher, so we cut out the affected section and replace it with Type L copper or a press fitting. More pinholes often follow.

    Preventive Copper Pipe Maintenance in Asheville, NC

    Copper gives warning signs before it leaks. They are easy to overlook. Green crust at fittings, a stain on a basement ceiling and high static pressure all point to trouble ahead, so we look over the pipe you can see and test the pressure. A regulator often helps.

    Copper Leak Behind Walls in Asheville, NC

    Small pinholes can suddenly widen and spray water into a wall or ceiling. Locate your shutoff. We help you shut off the water over the phone and send a plumber to locate and repair the leak, and if the damage is extensive, we make a safe temporary fix to restore water while we plan the full repair. We tell you about wet drywall.

    Underground Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Asheville

    Buried copper service lines can leak for months before anyone notices. The meter gives it away. We locate the leak with acoustic gear and tracer gas, then repair it through a small pit or replace the line by pulling new PEX or Type K copper through the old path. Every repair gets a pressure test.

    Copper Slab Leak Repair in Asheville

    Many slab homes have copper water lines running under the concrete. Listen for running water. When those lines leak, you may notice warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water or a meter that turns with every fixture off, so we find it electronically first and then quote a slab repair or a reroute. We price each option.

    Commercial Copper Pipe Leak Services in Asheville, NC

    In a commercial building, one copper leak can affect several tenants at once. Shutdowns need coordination.     Pinhole Leak Repair in Asheville

    Pinholes start inside the pipe where the copper wall pits and thins. You see a drip or a stain. They are small and easy to miss behind drywall, which is why we use thermal imaging to find them before we open anything. We replace the pitted length with Type L. We look at the copper around it.
