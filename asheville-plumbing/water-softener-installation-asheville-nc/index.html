    Whole-Home Water Softener Installation in Asheville

    Whole-home softeners treat every gallon that enters the house, right after the main shutoff. That protects every faucet, shower, water heater and appliance downstream, which is where hard water does most of its damage over the years. Sizing comes first. Hardness and household use set the grain capacity.

    Whole-House Water Softener Systems in Asheville

    On a whole-house system, the softener goes on the main line after the shutoff and before the water heater. Every whole-house install gets a bypass valve, and we add a pressure reducing valve if incoming pressure is too high. Every tap gets soft water. We can skip the hose bibs.

    Pelican Water Softener Installation in Asheville, NC

    Pelican sells both salt-based softeners and salt-free conditioners, often direct to homeowners. When a homeowner has already bought one, we handle the plumbing, the bypass and the startup flush the manual requires. Startup matters. We follow it step by step.

    Salt-Based Water Softener Installation in Asheville

    Ion exchange is the only method that truly removes hardness from water. We install salt-based softeners with a resin tank sized to your grains per gallon, a brine tank, a bypass valve and a drain line that ends at a code-approved air gap. Programming is the last step. We set regeneration overnight.

    Reverse Osmosis Drinking Water Systems in Asheville

    Reverse osmosis pushes water through a fine membrane that removes most dissolved solids, including the sodium a softener adds. Our RO installs go under the sink with their own faucet, and we can tie in the fridge or ice maker too. Pairs well with a softener. Filters get changed on schedule.

    Salt-Based Water Softener Systems in Asheville, NC

    Salt-based softeners use ion exchange, swapping calcium and magnesium for sodium on a bed of resin beads. HFN Plumbing installs these with a properly sized resin tank and brine tank. We program regeneration by metered water use so the system only recharges when the resin is actually spent, which saves a lot of salt. Most effective option.
