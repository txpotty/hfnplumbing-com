    What Goes Into a Sewer Line Replacement in Asheville, NC

    Replacing a Collapsed Sewer by Trench in Asheville

    Collapsed pipe cannot be burst or lined. When the camera stops at a pile of broken clay or a flattened section of Orangeburg, the only fix is to dig down, clear the debris and lay new pipe through that stretch. Digging also lets us see the soil around the break. Often there is a void or a root mat right at the failure point.

    Trenchless Sewer Replacement in Asheville, NC

    Two holes instead of one long trench. With bursting, a steel head splits the old pipe into the soil and pulls new HDPE into the same path. The other option is a cured-in-place liner. A resin-soaked tube is pulled into the cleaned host pipe and cured in place, which leaves a new pipe inside the old one and works well when the original still holds its round shape.

    Sewer Permits and Code in Asheville, NC

    Sewer replacement nearly always needs a permit. Our office files the permit and books the inspection, and our crew meets the inspector so you do not have to. The inspector looks at pipe size, slope, bedding and cleanouts. Unpermitted sewer work can show up on a home inspection when you sell.

    Emergency Sewer Collapse Repair in Asheville

    Every drain in the house stops when a lateral collapses. The tech scopes the line, marks the break and lets you know whether the dig can start next day or has to wait on locates. Locates usually take a couple of business days. A temporary bypass can keep a bathroom working in the meantime.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Asheville

    A collapsed run or a long belly usually means we dig. We open a trench along the old line, pull the broken pipe and lay new PVC on compacted bedding at a steady fall of an eighth to a quarter inch per foot. Where we can, the sod is cut and set aside before the digging starts. The inspector sees open pipe. We compact the backfill in layers, which keeps a dip from forming along the trench.

    Upgrading Old Sewer Pipe Materials in Asheville

    Clay, cast iron and Orangeburg all fail in their own ways. Clay joints let roots in every few feet, cast iron scales and rusts through along the bottom, and Orangeburg tar paper pipe slowly flattens into an oval. PVC and HDPE do none of that. HDPE is fused into one continuous piece with no joints for roots to find.

    How Pipe Bursting Works in Asheville

    A winch pulls the bursting head. Because the head is a little wider than the old pipe, it cracks it into the surrounding dirt as the new HDPE follows. You get a pit at each end. The fused pipe has no joints, so roots cannot get in again.

    Most of our sewer work is single-family homes. Coastal areas have sandy soil and high water tables, which affect how sewer lines and septic systems perform.
