    What Goes Into a Sewer Replacement in Wake Forest, NC

    Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Wake Forest

    Cast iron sewer pipe lasts a long time, but not forever. Over decades it scales up inside and the bottom wears through. We replace it with PVC, and where cast iron remains under the house we use a shielded no-hub coupling at the transition. Clean seal.

    Advanced Diagnostics for Sewer Issues in Wake Forest, NC

    Diagnosis comes before price. Video tells us the problem and the sonde tells us the spot. We sometimes add a hydro-jet cleaning first, because a line full of grease can hide a crack, and a clean pipe gives a much more honest picture of its condition. Guesswork costs money.

    Sewer Cleanout Installation in Wake Forest, NC

    A lot of older houses have no outside cleanout. Without one, the plumber has to pull a toilet or go through the roof vent. When we replace a sewer line we add a two-way cleanout near the foundation, with a cap at ground level you can find, so future cleaning or camera work is quick and cheap.

    Addressing Tree Root Intrusions in Wake Forest, NC

    Roots find sewer lines by following moisture. A hairline crack is enough. Once inside, they grow into a mat that catches paper and grease, and cutting them out with a machine only buys a few months, because the opening is still there. New PVC with glued joints gives them no way in.

    Traditional Sewer Line Replacement in Wake Forest

    Digging is still the right method for a lot of lines. A collapsed pipe cannot guide a bursting head. Sharp bends, a belly that needs regrading, or a route that should change all point toward an open trench, and we leave the yard as close to how we found it as dirt allows. Grass grows back.

    Foundation Protection during Sewer Replacement in Wake Forest, NC

    Crawlspace foundations are very common here, which makes many plumbing repairs easier to reach than they would be under a slab. A sewer leaking under or beside the house keeps the soil wet in one spot, which can lead to uneven settling of the slab. When we replace a line near the foundation we dig carefully, avoid undermining footings, and tamp each layer of fill. Slow work near the house.

    Clay and Orangeburg Pipe Replacement in Wake Forest, NC

    Clay pipe came in short sections with a joint every two or three feet. Each of those joints is an opening for roots. Orangeburg is worse, since the tar-paper wall flattens slowly under the dirt above it, and both are good candidates for bursting because they break apart easily. Fast pulls.

    Trenchless Pipe Bursting in Wake Forest

    Bursting works best on clay, cast iron and Orangeburg. A hydraulic rig at the receiving pit pulls the bursting head through the old line, splitting it into the soil, while a continuous length of fused HDPE follows behind so the new pipe has no joints at all between the pits. Roots cannot get into a joint that does not exist.
