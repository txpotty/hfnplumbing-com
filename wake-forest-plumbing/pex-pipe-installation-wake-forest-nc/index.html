    What Goes Into a PEX Install in Wake Forest, NC

    PEX for Remodels & Additions in Wake Forest, NC

    Opening a wall for a kitchen remodel is the cheapest time to replace old supply lines. While the studs are exposed we can run PEX back to a manifold or a sound trunk, set fresh stub-outs for the sink and dishwasher, and add a fridge line. Additions get their own sizing. A far-end bathroom may need a three-quarter inch feed to hold pressure.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Wake Forest

    PEX-B is stiffer, costs less per foot, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. A crimp joint is only as good as the tool, so we check every ring with a go/no-go gauge, and a ring that does not pass gets cut out and redone. It is a five-second check. Skipping it causes most crimp leaks.

    New Construction PEX Plumbing in Wake Forest, NC

    On a new build, HFN Plumbing runs PEX supply lines while the framing is still open in Wake Forest, NC. Plates get drilled, the manifold goes up, and every stub-out lands at the plan heights before the rough-in inspection and its pressure test. Nail plates go on wherever pipe crosses a stud. Builders like the pace.

    Remodel and Addition PEX Rough-In in Wake Forest

    Rough-in heights matter more than people think. A shower valve set two inches too low or a vanity stub that misses the cabinet back means opening the wall again after the tile is up, which is expensive and slow. We confirm heights with your contractor first. Then we pressure test with the walls open.

    Manifold and Home-Run PEX Layouts in Wake Forest, NC

    Think of a manifold as a breaker panel for water. A central header feeds each fixture on its own line with its own valve, which lets you shut off one toilet and leave the kitchen running. You buy more pipe. The trade-off is almost no fittings hidden in the walls and steadier pressure when two showers run at once.

    Copper to PEX Conversions in Wake Forest

    Pinhole leaks in copper tend to come in clusters. Aggressive water, fast flow and soil contact under slabs can pit copper from the inside, and the first few pinholes are typically followed by more. Municipal water in North Carolina is generally soft, but many private wells produce acidic water that can eat away at copper pipe over time. Converting to PEX stops the cycle. We use listed transition fittings anywhere PEX ties into copper that stays.

    Full Home PEX Repiping in Wake Forest

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside out. After 40 or 50 years the bore is so choked with scale that a half-inch line delivers a trickle, and the water comes out tinted brown after it sits overnight. A full PEX repipe replaces all of it. Pressure comes back at the fixtures and the rust stops.

    Leak Repair with PEX in Wake Forest, NC

    A single bad section is not always a reason to repipe. We can cut out a split or corroded stretch of copper, transition to PEX with a listed fitting, and have the water back on in an hour or two. That works for one leak. Once you are on the third patch, we will tell you straight whether the rest of the copper is heading the same way.
