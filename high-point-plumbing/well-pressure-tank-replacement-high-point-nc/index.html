    Pressure Switch Replacement in High Point, NC

    Switch contacts pit and burn over time. A pitted switch can stick on or chatter. When we swap a tank we look at the contacts and the sensing port underneath, and we usually recommend replacing a switch older than the tank it served. Cheap part.

    Large Capacity Tanks for Ranch and Commercial Wells in High Point

    Big properties need big drawdown. Stock tanks, irrigation and barns add up. We install large capacity tanks or banks of tanks on a manifold, sizing them so the pump runs long enough each cycle even under heavy demand. Floor space matters.

    Diaphragm Pressure Tank Replacement in High Point, NC

    Diaphragm tanks use a flexible sheet clamped inside the shell. Air sits above it, water below. When it fails, symptoms match a bladder tank, with short cycling, pressure swings and water spitting from the air valve. Replacement is the usual answer.

    Pressure Switch and Gauge Integration in High Point, NC

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. It works with the tank, not apart from it. A switch set at 40/60 needs a tank pre-charged to 38 psi, and a gauge that reads correctly so you can see what the system is doing, so we replace a worn gauge rather than trusting a sticky needle. Small parts, big role.

    Tank Tee, Gauge and Relief Valve Replacement in High Point

    The tank tee is the brass manifold that connects tank, switch, gauge, drain and relief valve. Threads on old tees weep. The relief valve should open before the system reaches a dangerous pressure, and a corroded valve may not open when needed. Whole set, one visit.

    Commercial Well Pressure Tank Services in High Point, NC

    Old galvanized tanks have no bladder at all. Water sits right against an air pocket. Over time the water absorbs the air, the tank waterlogs, which means draining it to restore the air. A sealed bladder tank ends that chore.
