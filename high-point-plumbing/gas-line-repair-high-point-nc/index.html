    What Our Gas Line Plumbers Fix in High Point, NC

    Gas Line Extensions and Relocations in High Point

    Adding a gas fireplace, moving a laundry room or switching a range from electric to gas usually means extending the gas line. Sizing comes first. We add up the BTU load of every appliance on the system, measure the longest run and pick a pipe size that keeps pressure up at the farthest appliance when everything runs together. Then we route it, tie it in and test it.

    Natural Gas Leak Detection and Repair in High Point

    Finding a gas leak starts at the meter. We shut off every appliance and watch the meter's test dial, then close branch valves one at a time to find which section is leaking. A combustible gas detector narrows it further. A spray of soap solution on the fitting shows bubbles at the leak, and we fix that joint or section and retest the whole system.

    Interior Black Iron Pipe Repair in High Point

    Threaded black iron pipe can leak at joints after years of vibration or settling. We tighten, reseal or replace fittings and sections with new pipe and gas-rated thread sealant. Rusty runs get swapped out. Pipe in a garage, crawlspace or exterior wall that shows heavy rust gets replaced before it turns into a leak, and the whole system gets a pressure test after the repair.

    CSST Repair and Bonding in High Point, NC

    Newer homes often have corrugated stainless steel tubing running through the attic. Nails, rodents and lightning can all damage CSST, and older yellow-jacket tubing must be bonded to the home's grounding system to lower arc risk. It takes about an hour. We repair damaged sections with manufacturer-approved fittings and verify bonding before we leave. Hurricanes and tropical storms can bring heavy rain and flooding to North Carolina, especially in coastal and low-lying areas.

    Cost of Gas Line Repair in High Point, NC

    Gas line repair prices in High Point, NC depend on where the leak is and how much pipe has to change. Small fixes stay in the low hundreds. Trenching and replacing a buried line often lands between $1,000 and $5,000, with length, soil and any concrete in the path setting the final number. We put the price in writing first.

    Gas Appliance Connector Repair in High Point, NC

    Weak flames or a smell near one appliance often point to the connector or shutoff behind it. Uncoated brass connectors from decades ago crack at the flare fittings and should be replaced on sight. We replace them with coated stainless connectors. We also add a reachable shutoff and a drip leg where needed, and confirm the new connector is rated for that appliance's BTU input.

    Pool Heater, Fire Pit and Grill Lines in High Point, NC

    Backyard gas lines for pool heaters, fire pits and built-in grills face sun, weather and irrigation water that shorten their life. Pool heaters are the big load, often 250,000 to 400,000 BTU, so a line that was fine for a grill can starve the heater and the house appliances at the same time. We size outdoor lines for the full load. Repairs and new runs are tested, buried at code depth and marked with tracer wire.

    Propane Gas Line Service in High Point, NC

    Propane systems run at different pressures than natural gas and use a two-stage regulator setup between the tank and the house. Regulators fail too. We repair buried and above-ground propane lines, replace leaking regulators and fittings and check that each appliance has been converted with the correct orifices for propane. A natural gas burner running on propane will burn rich and soot up. We test the system the same way we test natural gas piping.
