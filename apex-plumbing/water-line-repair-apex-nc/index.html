    What Our Water Line Repair Covers in Apex, NC

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Apex, NC

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Municipal water in North Carolina is generally soft, but many private wells produce acidic water that can corrode copper pipe over time. Scale builds up inside and the opening shrinks. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, and so is pressure that falls off when the shower and the washer run together. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Most homes are done in a day.

    Trenchless Water Line Repair in Apex

    Trenchless work makes sense when the line runs under a driveway, patio or a row of mature shrubs. We dig a pit at the meter and another at the house. The new line travels between them without a trench. A pneumatic boring tool or a cable puller drags HDPE or PEX through the soil, or through the old pipe if it is a straight run, usually in one working day from start to backfill.

    Commercial Water Line Repair in Apex, NC

    Commercial service lines are often 1 1/2 to 4 inch pipe feeding a meter bank, a backflow assembly and sometimes a fire line. No water means no open sign. HFN Plumbing can schedule repairs early in the morning or after closing, and we bring the larger fittings and couplings on the truck. We also arrange the backflow test and inspection that most water providers require after work on the commercial side of the meter.

    Copper Water Line Repair in Apex

    Buried copper is tough, but it is not forever. Rocks pressing against the pipe wear pinholes over time, and acidic soil or high water velocity can thin the wall from the inside. A two-foot section is typical. The new piece goes in with soldered or pressed couplings, and we check the exposed pipe on both sides for green crust or soft spots.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Apex

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Older homes around Apex may have galvanized steel supply lines or cast iron drains that are reaching the end of their life. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. Acoustic gear and a thermal camera tell us where it is. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. You get a price on every option.

    Emergency Water Line Repair in Apex, NC

    First thing on a burst line is shutting the water off, at the house valve if it works and at the curb stop if it does not. Free-running water washes soil out from under walks and slabs fast. HFN Plumbing sends a plumber with a curb key, a pump and repair couplings. We can usually stop it within the hour. If the repair will run long, we rig a temporary bypass so the house has water while we work.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Apex

    Underground leaks hide well. Many people find out from the bill before they see anything in the yard. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or a spot near the walk that stays muddy days after rain. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. The dig follows the sound.

    Underground Leak Detection in Apex, NC

    A ground microphone picks up the hiss of water escaping a pressurized pipe, even through a couple of feet of dirt. Some leaks barely make a sound. For those we push a hydrogen and nitrogen tracer gas into the drained line and sniff for it at the surface, which finds leaks the microphone cannot hear. Crawlspace foundations are very common here, which makes many plumbing repairs more accessible than they would be under a slab. You get a written price as soon as the leak is marked on the ground.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Apex

    A minor leak repair often starts around $500. Replacing the whole line is a bigger number. A meter-to-house replacement or a trenchless pull can reach $5,000 or more on a long run or a deep line, and the price moves with pipe type, depth and whether we trench or bore. We quote it in writing after the inspection.

    Age is the biggest cause. Old pipe corrodes. Shifting soil, tree roots, high water pressure and freeze-thaw cycles all add stress, and an old line gets damaged by excavation work more often than most people realize. Poor original installation is another, like rocks dumped right on the pipe.

    Most jurisdictions in North Carolina require a permit for a full service line replacement. Small repairs depend on the city. The permit usually comes from the city or county building department, with an inspection of the exposed pipe and bedding before we cover it. HFN Plumbing handles that paperwork for you.

    For PEX we use Uponor and Viega, depending on the job. Copper is Type K for buried runs. Couplings and transition fittings come from makers like Mueller and Ford, chosen for the ground and water conditions on your lot. We do not use cheap knockoff fittings.

    A typical spot repair runs two to four hours, including the locate and backfill. Full replacements are a day. Deep lines, long runs and jobs under concrete or pavers can push the job into a second or third day. You get a realistic timeline when we hand you the quote.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. A cable goes through the old pipe. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, and new HDPE pipe follows right behind it into the same space. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, which most residential service lines are.

    It means replacing the buried line without digging a trench along it. You get two pits instead of a trench. A boring tool or pipe puller drags new HDPE or PEX between them, by splitting the old line in place or boring a new path next to it. It is a good fit for lines under driveways, patios and established gardens in Apex.

    Often the pipe itself is not covered. Resulting damage sometimes is. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their insurer or the water provider, which can pay for the repair or replacement of the buried line itself. We provide photos and an itemized invoice for your claim.
