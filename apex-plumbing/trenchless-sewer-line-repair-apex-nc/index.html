    Orangeburg and Clay Pipe Repair in Apex

    Orangeburg is bituminous fiber pipe that was laid in houses mostly from the late 1940s into the early 1970s. Over time it softens, goes oval and blisters on the inside, so a liner has nothing round to bond to and bursting is usually the better fix. Clay is different. Clay that still holds its round shape is a good candidate for a liner.

    Causes of Sewer Line Failure in Apex, NC

    Shifting soil is one of the main reasons sewer joints pull apart or crack. Dense red clay is common in central parts of North Carolina, and it holds water and shifts with the seasons, stressing buried pipes. Roots are the other big one, since a hairline gap at a clay joint is enough for a feeder root to get in and then swell into a mat that catches paper. Then age finishes the job. Cast iron scales and thins, and clay bells crack under load.
