    PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in Apex, NC

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A is the most flexible and joins with expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and joins with crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C sits closer to B in feel. All three are code-listed for drinking water. We match the pipe to the fittings and tools from one maker so the warranty stays intact.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in Apex

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool and a cold-expansion ring. The tech expands the pipe and ring together, pushes the fitting in, and the plastic shrinks back around the barb over the next few seconds, tighter than when it started. No crimp tool to calibrate. Because the fittings are wide inside, each joint costs very little flow.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Apex, NC

    Gray polybutylene went into millions of homes from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. Chlorinated water breaks the pipe and its plastic fittings down from within, which means it can look perfectly fine until a fitting suddenly fails. Many insurers ask whether you have it. Swapping it for PEX can help with insurance and with resale.

    Full Home PEX Repiping in Apex

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside out. You get real pressure back, and the discolored water goes away with the old pipe.

    Whole-Home PEX Repiping in Apex

    Low pressure at every faucet, not just one, usually points at the pipe and not the fixtures.     PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Apex

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Either one lasts for decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.
