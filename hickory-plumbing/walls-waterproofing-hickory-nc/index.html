    How We Keep Water Out of Walls in Hickory, NC

    Interior Walls Waterproofing in Hickory, NC

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and tie it into a sump pit so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. The difference is that the water no longer ends up on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and works where a driveway or deck blocks the wall.

    Basement Wall Sealing in Hickory, NC

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. High humidity can corrode exposed metal pipe and fittings under homes, so crawlspace plumbing deserves a regular look. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then coat the face with a masonry waterproofing product that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. Most homeowners notice the musty smell is gone within a few weeks. The weep holes empty into interior drain tile when there is one.

    Commercial Wall Waterproofing in Hickory

    Restaurant kitchens, gym locker rooms and salon wash stations put water on walls all day. We waterproof commercial showers, mop sink walls and restroom wet walls with membranes listed for continuous wet use, and we repair the supply and drain piping behind them if demolition turns up a leak. We can work after hours.

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in Hickory

    Tile and grout are not waterproof. Water passes through grout every time you shower, so the layer behind the tile, a membrane over cement board or a foam board system, is what actually keeps the studs dry. We tear out failed tile, install new backer, and apply Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban at the thickness the manufacturer calls for. Corners, niches and valve holes get extra fabric or preformed pieces.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Hickory

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and usually add a dehumidifier to hold humidity under 60 percent. High humidity can rust exposed metal pipe and fittings under homes, so crawlspace plumbing deserves a regular look. The joists stop rotting. Drier air under the floor also cuts down on the musty smell in the house.

    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Hickory, NC

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Older homes around Hickory may have galvanized steel supply lines or cast iron drains that are wearing out. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. Sealing soft old brick with a rigid coating traps water inside it and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with interior drain tile. Old mortar joints get repointed first.

    Crawl Space Vapor Barriers in Hickory

    Bare dirt in a crawl space releases gallons of water vapor a day into the air under your floor. A 10 mil or heavier polyethylene barrier, lapped 12 inches at the seams and taped, cuts that off and keeps the joists and subfloor dry. We run it up the foundation walls and fasten it with termination bar. Cheap fix, big difference.

    Exterior Block Wall Waterproofing in Hickory, NC

    White crust on block or stem walls is efflorescence, salt left behind as water moves through the masonry and dries. The usual cause is a sprinkler head aimed at the house instead of the bed, or soil graded toward the foundation. We fix the water first. After that the wall gets cleaned, dried and coated with a breathable masonry sealer or elastomeric coating.

    Crack repairs take a few hours. Interior drain tile usually takes one to three days, and a full exterior dig around the whole house can run three to seven days or more depending on the weather and the depth of the footing. Your timeline comes with the quote.

    It depends on the wall and the site.     Simple crack injection usually does not need one.     Interior systems manage water after it has come through the wall, with drain tile along the footing and a sump to pump it out.     Damp proofing is usually a thin spray-on asphalt coat. It slows soil moisture but cannot hold back water under hydrostatic pressure. Waterproofing uses thicker membranes, drainage boards and footing drains rated to stop liquid water, which is what a basement in wet soil or a high water table needs.
