    Vessel and Grid Drain Replacement in Jacksonville

    Vessel sinks use a different drain. Most glass and stone vessel bowls have no overflow hole, so they need an unslotted grid or umbrella drain, and a slotted drain in one of those bowls leaks out of the slots right into the counter hole. We see the wrong drain often. Swapping it takes under an hour.

    Corroded Drain Replacement in Jacksonville, NC

    Drain cleaners speed up corrosion. Chemicals left sitting in a metal trap eat through it, so many old traps we pull have pinholes at the bottom. PVC does not have that problem. We recommend it for most cabinets.

    Utility and Basement Sink Drain Replacement in Jacksonville

    Basement sinks often drain to a pump. Sinks below the sewer line drain through a pump, so we check its valve and discharge line during the job. Pumps fail quietly. A gurgling pit is a clue.

    P-Trap Replacement in Jacksonville

    Thin chrome traps are the ones we replace most. They rust inside and their nuts seize, so we cut them out and fit heavy-wall PVC or polished brass. The new trap gets a full flow test. Total time is often under an hour.

    Bathroom Drain Upgrade in Jacksonville, NC

    Old bath drains often carry decades of hair and scum. A new pop-up and trap get rid of the rough walls hair clings to, and the bowl empties in seconds. Repeat clogs drop off. A hair catcher keeps it that way.

    Leaking P-Trap Replacement in Jacksonville

    Traps leak at the joints first. The beveled washers inside the slip nuts flatten and harden over the years, and a trap that has been taken apart several times to clear clogs often has stripped plastic threads or a cracked nut. A new trap kit costs little. Labor is most of the bill.

    Pop-Up Drain Replacement in Jacksonville

    Lift-rod pop-ups are the most common bath drain. We pull the old body, clean the flange seat down to bare porcelain, reset the new flange in putty or silicone, and adjust the clevis so the lift rod opens and closes the stopper with one easy motion. Finish matches the faucet. Overflow-style drains are standard.

    Galvanized drain arms rust from the inside. An old steel stub can crumble at the threads, so we cut it back and join new PVC with a shielded coupling. We handle cast iron the same way. Sometimes that means opening the wall.
