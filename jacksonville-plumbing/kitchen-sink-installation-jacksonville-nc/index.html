    Stainless Steel Sink Installation in Jacksonville

    Stainless sinks come in gauges from 16 to 22, and a lower gauge means thicker steel. Thin 22 gauge bowls flex and drum when the disposal runs, so we add sound pads or suggest an 18 gauge or heavier sink if you have not bought one yet. Top-mount and undermount plumb differently. Either way, we hook up the faucet, strainers and disposal.

    Apron-Front Sinks in Jacksonville, NC

    Apron sinks need a cabinet cut to fit the front panel, and the sink has to be in place before the counter is templated. We set the sink and frame early, so the fabricator measures from the actual bowl instead of a spec sheet. That avoids an uneven reveal. A cast iron apron sink can weigh 150 pounds or more, so two of us show up.

    Double Bowl Sink and Continuous Waste in Jacksonville

    A double bowl drains both sides through one trap, using either a center outlet continuous waste or an end outlet tee. The layout depends on your disposal and dishwasher, because the disposal side changes where the tee sits and how much slope each bowl gets. We use heavy-wall fittings.

    Drop-In Kitchen Sink Installation in Jacksonville

    Sitting on the counter by their rim, drop-in sinks are a natural fit for tile, laminate and butcher block counters. Usually the fastest upgrade. After checking the cutout, we seal the rim and draw the clips down evenly, and in older homes and rentals it is often a two-hour job.

    Undermount Sinks on Stone in Jacksonville, NC

    Stone shops vary on how they mount undermounts. Some epoxy threaded studs into the stone, others use a rail system, and we work with whichever one your countertop shop prefers. The bowl is braced regardless. We do not connect the trap until the adhesive has cured.

    Drop-In Sink Replacement in Jacksonville

    Pulling an old drop-in starts with cutting the caulk line and backing off the clips from below. Old clips are often rusted solid, so we bring a reciprocating saw and cut them off rather than prying on the counter. Laminate chips if you pry. The new sink needs to cover the old cutout fully.

    Composite Sink Installation in Jacksonville

    Granite and quartz composite sinks resist scratches on the surface but can chip at the drain hole if a strainer is overtightened. Hand tight comes first. Most composite brands want silicone rather than putty under the strainer because putty can stain them, so we go by the maker's instructions.

    New Construction Sink Rough-In in Jacksonville, NC

    Rough-in sets the drain height and the stops long before the cabinets arrive. We usually set the kitchen drain stub around 16 to 20 inches off the floor, then adjust for deep bowls or a disposal so the trap does not end up below the wall outlet. Venting happens at this stage. We work from your sink and faucet specs to avoid moving pipe later.
