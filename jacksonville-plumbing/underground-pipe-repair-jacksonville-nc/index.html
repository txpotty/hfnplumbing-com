    Frequently Asked Questions About Underground Pipe Repair in Jacksonville

    A single repair on a buried water line typically costs $800 to $2,500 including locating and excavation. A new service runs higher. Main water service replacement typically runs $2,500 to $6,500, and sewer lateral repairs range from $1,500 to $5,000 depending on how deep the pipe sits. You get the price on paper.

    Acoustic ground microphones pick up the sound of escaping water. Tracer gas helps too. Hydrogen-nitrogen gas pushed into the line rises through the soil at the leak, and electronic locators trace the exact pipe route so we know where to listen. Together they narrow it to a few feet.

    Check the meter with every fixture off. Look at the leak indicator. If it moves, water is going somewhere, and a wet or sinking spot in the yard or a hissing sound near the meter box usually points to the buried service. We can confirm it fast.

    Most point repairs take a few hours to a day. Replacements take longer. A full service line replacement typically takes one to two days, depending on length, depth and how much of the route can be done with a pull-through method. We give you the timeline first.

    Main water service and sewer replacements usually do. Small fixes may not. The Jacksonville building department typically wants a permit for any major buried line replacement, which allows an inspection before backfill. The permit is our job.

    Look for wet ground that never dries. Pressure drops matter too. Unexplained bill increases, foul odors near the cleanout or gurgling drains point to trouble in the pipe you cannot see. We can check it fast.

    Small repairs often do not. Full lines typically need one. Full water service or sewer line replacement usually requires a permit, and any work in the public right-of-way always needs coordination with the city. The permit is on us.

    For water lines we use PEX or Type K copper. Each is approved for direct bury. Sewer and drain work uses Schedule 40 PVC, and trenchless jobs use fused HDPE, and every one of these holds up to soil and water far better than galvanized or clay. Local code decides the final pick.
