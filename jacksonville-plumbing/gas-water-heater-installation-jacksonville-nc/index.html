    Power Vent Gas Water Heater Installation in Jacksonville, NC

    Power vent heaters use a blower to push exhaust through PVC or other approved pipe. Without needing a chimney, the vent can exit through a side wall within the allowed length. A 120-volt outlet is required. Termination clearances follow the manual.

    High-Efficiency Gas Water Heater Installation in Jacksonville, NC

    High-efficiency gas heaters use condensing technology to pull extra heat out of the exhaust. Because the exhaust comes out cool and wet, they vent through PVC and need a condensate drain, which changes where the heater can go and what the install involves. Energy Star models qualify for some rebates. We install Rheem, Bradford White and AO Smith models.

    Brands We Install in Jacksonville

    Rheem, Bradford White, AO Smith, Navien and Rinnai make up most of the gas heaters we install in Jacksonville homes and businesses. Each brand has different warranty lengths, recovery rates and vent options, and we lay those out side by side so you can pick based on facts. Price is part of it. We install all of them.

    Traditional Tank Gas Water Heater Installation in Jacksonville

    Tank gas heaters are still the workhorse in most garages and utility rooms, usually 40 or 50 gallons. Each install gets a new gas connector and drip leg, a vent connector pitched upward and a relief line piped where code allows. Strapping where required. Sizing follows household demand.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Jacksonville, NC

    Gas tankless units fire at up to 199,000 BTU, far more than a 40,000 BTU tank burner. That load usually calls for a 3/4 inch gas line or larger and a dedicated vent in PVC or stainless steel, depending on whether the unit is condensing. Endless hot water follows. Municipal water in North Carolina is generally soft, but many private wells produce acidic water that can corrode copper pipe over time. We recommend pre-filtration based on your water.

    Standard Tank Gas Water Heater Installation in Jacksonville

    Our standard gas tank install starts with removing the old heater. The old tank gets drained and removed, then the new one goes in with new connectors, a drip leg and a sloped vent. Then we test it. Value and reliability are why people choose them.
