    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer in Jacksonville

    Testing in Jacksonville, NC usually costs between $75 and $150 per assembly. The price includes the trip, a calibrated test and sending the report to your provider. Several devices at one address usually get a lower per-unit price because the tester is already set up. Commercial lines cost more.

    Ten to twenty years is common for a well-kept brass assembly. How long it lasts depends on the water, the weather it sits in and the kind of device. Hard freezes are uncommon along the coast but happen most winters in the mountains, where pipes in crawlspaces and outside walls need protection. Yearly testing extends its life.

    New installations and replacements often require a plumbing permit and an initial test. Each city and provider sets its own rules. We pull permits and handle the initial test, so the paperwork is done before the assembly goes into regular service.

    Once a year is the usual requirement. Installation, repair and relocation can each trigger a test too. Your provider's notice will list the deadline, and we can usually test within a few days of seeing it, sometimes sooner.

    Constant dripping from the relief valve, low water pressure and visible cracks are the usual signs. Odd-colored water is another sign. Prompt repair keeps you in compliance and keeps the water provider from sending a second notice with a new deadline attached.

    We service reduced pressure zone assemblies, double check valve assemblies, pressure vacuum breakers and atmospheric vacuum breakers. Watts, Wilkins, Febco and Conbraco are the most common, and we also see Apollo and Zurn on commercial work. Kits for most are on the truck.

    Most water providers, including those around Jacksonville, require a test annually. Many also want a test after installation, after any repair and whenever the assembly gets relocated. Check your notice for the deadline.

    An RPZ has two checks with a relief valve between them, and it is rated for high-hazard lines. A DCVA has two checks and no relief valve, so it is used for lower hazards like many fire lines and some irrigation systems. Local rules pick the device.
