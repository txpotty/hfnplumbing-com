    Toilet Replacement Cost Comparison in Jacksonville, NC

    Three repair calls in a year on the same toilet usually cost more than replacing it, especially when the toilet is old enough to waste water on every flush. You get repair and replacement prices next to each other. You make the call, and we do not push replacement on a toilet that only needs a flapper.

    Cracked Bowl or Tank Replacement in Jacksonville

    Sometimes only the tank is cracked, and if the model is still in production, a matching tank costs less than a whole new toilet. We check the model number first. A cracked bowl always means a full replacement, since the bowl carries the seal to the floor and cannot be patched.

    High-Efficiency Toilet (HET) Upgrades in Jacksonville, NC

    High-efficiency toilets carry a WaterSense label and a MaP score, which tells you how many grams of waste the toilet clears in one flush. Look for a score of at least 800. Higher numbers mean fewer double flushes and fewer plunger trips, and that is where the real water savings come from.

    Commercial Flush Valve Upgrades in Jacksonville

    Manual flush valves on older commercial toilets leak at the handle and stick open, and swapping to a sensor valve during a replacement improves hygiene without changing the rough-in. You can pick battery or hardwired sensors. Work can happen after hours, and we check that the new fixtures meet accessibility requirements.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Replacement in Jacksonville

    Wall-hung toilets mount to a steel carrier inside the wall, so replacing one means checking the carrier bolts, the gasket on the outlet and the condition of the wall finish around it. The carrier itself sometimes needs new bolts or a coupling. The new bowl gets matched to the carrier already in the wall whenever possible, which avoids opening the wall.

    Toilet Brands We Replace in Jacksonville

    Brand matters less than model, because every major maker sells a great toilet and a mediocre one at nearly the same price. Our picks come down to flush valve size and the MaP score. Parts availability counts too. A toilet with proprietary parts can turn a simple repair into a special order later.

    ADA and Commercial Toilet Replacement in Jacksonville, NC

    Public restrooms need bowls rated for heavy use, and many need to meet ADA height and clearance rules when a fixture is replaced. Clearances get checked before we order anything. Adding a sensor flush valve during the swap takes little extra time, and they cut down on unflushed bowls.

    Older toilets use 3.5 to 7 gallons per flush, and a WaterSense toilet uses 1.28 or less, so a household of four can save thousands of gallons a year. You will see it on the next water bill.

    Cracked porcelain is the clearest one, followed by clogs that come back every week, a base that rocks again after every reset, and a tank that uses 3.5 gallons or more. At that stage, more repairs are throwing good money after bad. We will say so if a repair still works.

    Municipal water in North Carolina is generally soft, but many private wells produce acidic water that can eat away at copper pipe over time. Hard water leaves calcium scale in the rim jets and on the flush valve seat, which slowly weakens the flush and makes flappers leak. A fully glazed trapway resists scale better. We recommend models with fully glazed trapways and simple fill valves.

    A handle that needs jiggling, a tank that refills on its own, a hiss after the flush or a flush that stops halfway all point to worn tank parts. Those can be replaced one at a time. Once a toilet is 25 years old, a full replacement usually beats rebuilding it.
