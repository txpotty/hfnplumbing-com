    Recirculation Systems for Tankless Heaters in Chapel Hill

    Tankless heaters can take a few extra seconds to get hot water to a far bathroom, because the unit has to sense flow and fire first. A recirc pump on a timer or sensor keeps the hot line primed, cutting the wait at the far sink from about a minute to a few seconds. Less water goes down the drain.

    Tankless vs Tank Cost Comparison in Chapel Hill, NC

    Upfront, a tankless install in Chapel Hill, NC costs more than a standard tank because of the unit price, the gas line upgrade and the venting changes. The gap shrinks over time. Less standby loss, a life of up to 20 years versus roughly 10 for a tank, and replaceable parts all count over the years. We price both side by side.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Chapel Hill, NC

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Chapel Hill, NC homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. Whole-house electric units pull a lot of current. Some need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, and a 100-amp panel often cannot carry that on top of the air conditioner and range. Panel capacity gets checked up front.

    Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Chapel Hill, NC

    Replacing an old tankless unit is usually simpler than a first-time conversion. The gas line is already sized and the vent path already exists, so in many homes it is a matter of matching the connections, checking the vent for damage and hanging the new unit. Vent sizes can change by brand. We confirm fit first.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Installation in Chapel Hill, NC

    Rinnai has been making tankless heaters for decades, and replacement parts are widely stocked. We install both condensing and non-condensing Rinnai models, and the right one depends on where the vent can go and how much you want to spend up front. The non-condensing ones need stainless vent pipe. Condensing units vent in PVC and drain condensate.

    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Chapel Hill, NC

    Calcium scale is what kills most tankless units early. Municipal water in North Carolina is generally soft, but many private wells produce acidic water that can corrode copper pipe over time. Once scale coats the heat exchanger, efficiency drops and error codes start, and over time it can crack the exchanger and void the warranty. Flush it yearly. Service valves make it easy.
