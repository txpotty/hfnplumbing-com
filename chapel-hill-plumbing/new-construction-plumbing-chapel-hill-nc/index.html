    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Chapel Hill

    Bathroom count drives the cost more than square footage does. Basement homes can cost more when fixtures sit below the sewer and need an ejector pump. We price gas piping as its own line. A square-foot guess misses too much, so we bid from plans.

    Rough-in on a typical house takes one to two weeks of crew time, spread over the build. Underground runs a day or two before the pour. Top-out happens after framing and takes three to five days, then trim waits until cabinets and counters are in, which can be months later on a custom home. Trim is one or two days.

    Coastal areas have sandy soil and high water tables, which affect how sewer lines and septic systems function. Ground movement puts stress on pipes under a slab. We bed drain lines in sand or gravel, sleeve every pipe that passes through concrete, and use flexible PEX for the water service where code allows it. Sleeves are cheap.

    We install Moen, Kohler, Delta and American Standard, plus most other major brands a builder or designer specifies. Homeowner-supplied fixtures are fine. We ask that they arrive on site before trim day so we can open the boxes and check for missing parts, because a missing cartridge or wrong finish can push trim back a week.

    Mountain areas see much colder winters than the coast, so plumbing needs differ quite a bit from one end of the state to the other. Hard freezes are uncommon along the coast but happen most winters in the mountains, where pipes in crawlspaces and outside walls require protection. We pick pipe routes and insulation to match. That can mean keeping supply lines out of exterior walls, insulating attic runs, and setting frost-free hose bibs that drain back inside the heated space. Attics get the most attention.

    Workmanship is covered under our written warranty, and the length is spelled out in your contract. Manufacturer warranties cover the fixtures themselves. PEX and water heater warranties can run ten years or longer if the matching fittings are used, so we keep each system one brand.

    PEX-A is our default for water supply in North Carolina homes. It handles heat well and resists the scale and pinhole problems copper can get with aggressive water. Drains are Schedule 40 PVC. Under slabs, we keep joints to a minimum and use long-sweep fittings at every change of direction so a cable or camera can get through later.

    Expect plumbing to touch the schedule three times. Underground takes one to three days. Top-out usually takes three to five days on a single-family house, and trim takes one or two days once cabinets, counters and tile are in place. Inspections add a day each.
