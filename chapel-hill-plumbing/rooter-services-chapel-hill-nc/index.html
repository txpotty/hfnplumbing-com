    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Chapel Hill

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet typically runs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout generally runs $200 to $500. With hydro jetting added, expect roughly $450 to $1,000, depending on how long the line is and how heavy the grease is. Nights and weekends run higher.

    No permit is required for routine rooting. A new cleanout or a pipe repair may need one. Permits for repair work are on us.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting is different. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the pipe walls clean. Many tough lines do best with both, cut first to open the line and jetted second to wash out what the blades left.

    Clay or older cast iron lines with trees nearby usually need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Newer PVC lines may go many years without it. A camera helps set the schedule. How quickly roots return after the last cut tells us if yearly or every-other-year service fits your line.

    Our trucks carry Spartan and RIDGID machines, plus jetters sized for residential and commercial lines. They hold up well to heavy daily use. Several sizes of cable and cutter ride on each truck, so the tech matches the tool to the pipe rather than using one setup everywhere.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. They can soften old PVC glue joints and pit cast iron over time. They also burn skin. A rooter cable removes the clog itself rather than eating a channel through it, so the line stays open far longer.

    Gurgling toilets, sewage odors and slow drains in several fixtures are the common signs. A tub that fills when you flush is a big one. That one is serious. Repeat clogs in one line usually mean a deeper problem, often roots at a joint well out in the yard.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in Chapel Hill's older homes take the most damage. They also make our job riskier. Let us know if you used a chemical, because that water splashes when the trap comes off.
