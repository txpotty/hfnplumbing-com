    Repiping and Pipe Replacement Choices in Chapel Hill, NC

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Chapel Hill, NC

    Galvanized steel was common before the 1960s. The zinc wears off and the steel rusts inside. Over decades the opening shrinks, pressure drops and the water turns brown after sitting, and the threads where pipes join are often the first to leak. Replacing it is the only fix.

    Whole-Home Re-Piping in Chapel Hill

    A whole-home repipe replaces every supply line from the main shutoff to each fixture, including the water heater connections. Most single-story homes take two to three days. We run new trunk lines through the attic or crawlspace, branch down to fixtures through small wall openings, with service restored each evening when possible. Then patching.

    Pipe Replacement for Multi-Unit Buildings in Chapel Hill

    Apartments and condos share risers. One leak can affect several units. We plan riser replacement in phases, one stack at a time, notify tenants ahead of shutoffs, and test each section before moving on. Organized work.

    Emergency Pipe Replacement in Chapel Hill, NC

    A burst pipe needs fast action. Close the main shutoff right away. Then call, because water spreading through walls and floors does damage every minute, and we can usually get there, stop the leak and replace the failed section the same day. Drying comes next.

    PEX Pipe Replacement in Chapel Hill

    PEX bends around corners and comes in long rolls, so a typical repipe needs fewer fittings than copper would on the same layout. Sudden cold snaps can catch North Carolina homeowners off guard, especially in homes with uninsulated crawlspace plumbing. PEX can stretch a little in a freeze instead of splitting. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings where possible, keeping flow strong at each joint. Faster install.

    Corroded Pipe Replacement in Chapel Hill, NC

    Corrosion shows up as green or white crust on copper, rust on steel, or pinholes that drip. Water chemistry plays a role. We replace corroded sections or the whole system depending on how widespread the damage is, including fittings near the heater. Look at the joints.

    Slab Leak Pipe Replacement in Chapel Hill

    Rerouting is often better than digging. Jackhammering floors is a last resort. We run new pipe through the attic or walls to bypass the leaking line under the concrete, and usually costs less than repairing in place. Fast too.

    PEX Repiping in Chapel Hill

    PEX repipes go faster than copper. Flexible lines snake through walls with fewer openings, and home-run systems from a manifold let you shut off individual fixtures without killing water to the house. Popular choice.
