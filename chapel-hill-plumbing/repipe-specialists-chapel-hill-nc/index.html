    What Our Repipe Crews Do in Chapel Hill, NC

    Copper Repiping in Chapel Hill, NC

    Some homeowners prefer copper for its long track record and rigidity. Our copper work uses thicker Type L pipe, joined with lead-free solder or ProPress fittings, rather than the Type M found in many older houses. Expect a slightly longer job. Copper repipes usually cost more and take a day longer than PEX, mostly because every turn needs a fitting.

    Slab Leak Repiping in Chapel Hill

    A slab leak shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a running meter or a wet patch of carpet with no visible cause. Dense red clay is common in central parts of North Carolina, and it holds water and shifts with the seasons, stressing buried pipes. Instead of breaking concrete to patch one pipe, we reroute new supply lines overhead through the attic and down interior walls, then abandon the old lines in the slab. No jackhammer needed. That stops the current leak and removes the chance of the next one under the same floor.

    PEX-A Whole-Home Repiping in Chapel Hill

    PEX-A is flexible, resists scale and handles thermal expansion well, which suits hard water and long attic runs. Our crews use Uponor or similar PEX-A, where the pipe end gets expanded and shrinks back over the fitting for a full-diameter connection. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. Two or three days covers most houses.

    Repiping for Hard Water in Chapel Hill, NC

    Well owners around Chapel Hill benefit from periodic water testing and, when needed, neutralizers or filters that protect pipes and appliances. Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe, and over years it narrows the bore and speeds up corrosion in metal lines. Pressure suffers. In hard water homes, PEX-A is often the better pick since it resists scale, and a softener loop or filter can be added during the job. Planning it now saves a second visit later.

    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Chapel Hill, NC

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. The pipe gets brittle from the inside, and fittings crack, so a home that has had one polybutylene leak is usually at risk everywhere else in the system. We replace the full system with PEX or copper. Insurers often ask about it at renewal.

    Emergency Pipe Failures and Repipes in Chapel Hill

    Sometimes a house reaches the repipe point all at once, with a burst line and two more leaks right behind it. We stop the active leak first, make temporary repairs that get water back on and then plan the full repipe so you are not rushed into it. That gives you time. Your policy may pay for water damage even if not for the new pipe, so we take photos of everything we find.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement for Older Homes in Chapel Hill, NC

    Older homes may still have galvanized steel pipe that rusts from the inside out. Low pressure, brown water and leaks at threaded joints are the typical signs. We remove or abandon the galvanized lines and replace them with PEX or copper, and your water pressure and water quality usually improve the same day the new lines go into service.
