    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Repair in Chapel Hill

    A small fitting repair in Chapel Hill can be around $250. Big jobs cost more. Replacing a buried line that needs a long trench, or a repair that requires breaking concrete, can run $5,000 or more depending on how much pipe changes and how hard it is to reach. You get the number in writing first.

    Utilities lock the meter when they find a leak or a failed test. Service stays off until a licensed plumber fixes it and the system passes a test.

    Gas lines do not need routine service the way a water heater does. Checks still help. On other service calls we can check exposed gas pipe, connectors and valves and flag old connectors, missing drip legs or rust before it becomes a leak.

    CSST is approved and widely used when installed and bonded correctly. We verify bonding and repair damaged sections with manufacturer-approved parts.

    Simple repairs take an hour or two. Buried yard line replacements often take a full day, plus inspection time if a permit is needed.

    We work on black iron, coated steel, CSST such as Gastite and TracPipe, and polyethylene for buried lines. Code decides where each one goes. PE stays underground with an approved riser at the house, CSST requires bonding, and black iron needs supports and the right thread compound.

    Our gas line repairs come with a written warranty on parts and labor, usually one year. Coverage varies by repair. The exact terms are listed on your estimate before work starts, and any new connectors, valves or CSST fittings also carry their manufacturer warranties.

    There is no required yearly inspection for most homes. Older homes benefit from a check every few years.
