    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Chapel Hill

    Setting a customer-supplied standard toilet usually costs $200 to $400 in labor. Add a mid-range toilet and the total is usually $450 to $900. Wall-hung systems with an in-wall carrier run $1,500 to $3,500 or more, mostly because the wall has to be opened, framed for the carrier and finished again afterward. We quote a flat price.

    About two hours for most floor-mount toilets. Wall-mounted and smart toilets take longer. Wall-mounted units usually need two visits, carrier first and bowl after the finish wall, and a smart toilet can hinge on the electrician.

    We guarantee the workmanship on every toilet we set. Any leak from our install is our problem. That covers the seal and the supply connection, while manufacturing defects in the toilet fall under the maker's warranty, which we help you file if a part fails early. Hang on to your receipt.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Most homes measure 12 inches. You will also see 10 and 14 inch rough-ins, and buying the wrong one means the toilet will not fit or sits well off the wall. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.

    Just about any brand you can buy at a supply house or home center. TOTO and American Standard are the ones we set most often. Gerber and Mansfield are common in rentals because parts are cheap and easy to get, and we can usually tell you which one fits your budget and your rough-in after a short call. Bring us the model number.

    Adding or moving a toilet almost always needs one. A simple swap usually goes without. The permit covers drain sizing and venting, and the inspector wants to see both before the floor or wall is closed, so we schedule the inspection around your remodel timeline.

    Cracks in the tank or bowl are the clearest sign. Hairline cracks can be hard to spot. Look for a slow drip on the floor that comes back after you dry it, or frequent clogs on an early low-flow model with a narrow trapway. So is a toilet you have rebuilt twice in three years.

    Figure one to two hours for a standard floor-mount toilet. Bigger jobs take a day or more. Wall-hung carriers and slab relocations open walls or floors, so they get booked as separate projects with cure time built in.
