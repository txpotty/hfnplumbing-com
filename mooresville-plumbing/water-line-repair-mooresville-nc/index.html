    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Mooresville

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Homes built from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s may still have polybutylene pipe, which is known for sudden failures. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. We pinpoint the leak with acoustic gear and thermal imaging. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. We price each one for you.

    Commercial Water Line Repair in Mooresville, NC

    Commercial service lines are often 1 1/2 to 4 inch pipe feeding a meter bank, a backflow assembly and sometimes a fire line. A break shuts the business down. HFN Plumbing can schedule repairs early in the morning or after closing, and we bring the larger fittings and couplings on the truck. We also arrange the backflow test and inspection that most water providers require after work on the commercial side of the meter.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Mooresville

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. PEX usually fails at a fitting. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, and for PEX we use the matching crimp or expansion tool so the joint is made the way the manufacturer intends.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Mooresville, NC

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. Once one spot leaks, the pipe on either side is usually just as thin. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, and sometimes that buys a few good years. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. You get your flow back as a bonus.

    After-Hours Water Line Breaks in Mooresville, NC

    Water lines tend to break on a Saturday night. Ours is a 24 hour line. HFN Plumbing sends a plumber who can shut off the curb stop, pump the hole and make a permanent repair on the spot, which saves you from a weekend on a temporary patch. If the break is on the utility side of the meter, we tell you right away and help you get the provider out. That happens more than you would think.

    Main Water Line Repair in Mooresville

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. If it still moves, the leak is buried. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, and we dig one hole instead of a trench across your yard.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Mooresville

    Underground leaks hide well. Many people find out from the bill before they see anything in the yard. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or ground that stays soft long after the sprinklers shut off. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. The dig follows the sound.

    Emergency Water Line Repair in Mooresville, NC

    First thing on a burst line is shutting the water off, at the house valve if it works and at the curb stop if it does not. Free-running water washes soil out from under walks and slabs fast. HFN Plumbing sends a plumber with a curb key, a pump and repair couplings. Most breaks are shut down quickly. If the repair will run long, a temporary bypass keeps water flowing to the house during the repair.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Mooresville

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. Depth drives most of that. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, and trenchless replacement often lands between $3,000 and $7,000 because of the equipment involved. The written price comes before we start.

    A typical spot repair runs two to four hours, from locating the leak to filling the hole. Replacements take longer. Deep lines, long runs and jobs under concrete or pavers can push the job into a second or third day. You get a realistic timeline when we hand you the quote.

    Spot repairs usually take a few hours. Plan on a day for a full replacement. Jobs with a deep line, a lot of hardscape or a trenchless pull sometimes run two or three days when concrete has to be cut and patched or the inspector cannot come right away. We give you a time estimate with the quote.

    A full service line replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. Spot repairs are often exempt. The rules come from your city or county, and they can differ even between neighboring towns in the same county. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when it is required.

    We work on copper, PEX, HDPE poly, PVC and old galvanized steel. We also run into gray polybutylene now and then. For new work in Mooresville, Type K copper and burial-rated PEX are our usual picks, with HDPE for pulled lines. The trucks carry transition fittings for every one of them.

    Most replacements get Type K copper or PEX. HDPE goes in on trenchless pulls. Copper lasts a very long time and resists punctures, while PEX handles ground movement and freezing better and costs less to install. We recommend one based on your soil, line length, budget and what your local code allows in Mooresville.

    Fix small leaks early. A drip becomes a gusher.
