    How We Keep Water Out of Walls in Mooresville, NC

    Basement Wall Sealing in Mooresville, NC

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. High humidity can rust exposed metal pipe and fittings under homes, so crawlspace plumbing deserves a regular look. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then seal the face with a masonry waterproofer that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. The musty smell usually fades within weeks. Those weep holes empty into the interior drain if the house has one.

    Below-Grade and Basement Wall Waterproofing in Mooresville

    Much of the housing in North Carolina was built after the 1970s, and many of those homes use CPVC, copper or PEX supply lines. Any wall below grade sits in soil that gets saturated after heavy rain or snowmelt, and that water pushes against the wall with real hydrostatic pressure. We inject cracks, coat the wall inside or out, and install drain tile where water pools along the footing. A sump goes in if groundwater sits high. Some basements never need a sump.

    Crawl Space Vapor Barriers in Mooresville

    Bare dirt in a crawl space releases gallons of water vapor a day into the air under your floor. A 10 mil or heavier polyethylene barrier, lapped 12 inches at the seams and taped, cuts that off and keeps the joists and subfloor dry. We run it up the foundation walls and fasten it with termination bar. It is a cheap fix that makes a big difference.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Mooresville

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and often add a dehumidifier to keep relative humidity below 60 percent. High humidity can corrode exposed metal pipe and fittings under homes, so crawlspace plumbing deserves a regular look. The joists stop rotting. Drier air under the floor also cuts down on the musty smell in the house.

    Foundation Crack Repair in Mooresville

    Crack injection takes about two hours per crack. We set injection ports every 8 to 12 inches along the crack, seal the surface with epoxy paste, and pump in resin starting at the lowest port and working up until it pushes out the next port. Polyurethane stays flexible if the wall moves a little, and epoxy bonds the two sides back together. The surface paste gets ground off once it cures.

    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Mooresville

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane foam, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. They can mean the wall is bowing from soil pressure, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Most vertical cracks get sealed in one visit.

    Sump Pump Installation and Repair in Mooresville, NC

    A sump pump is the part of an interior system that actually moves the water. We set a lidded basin below slab level, install a cast iron pump sized for the inflow, and add a battery backup or water-powered backup for the night the power goes out during a storm. Check valves matter. A missing check valve lets the discharge line drain back into the pit and the pump short-cycles. The discharge line gets routed at least 10 feet from the house.

    Shower Pan Leak Repair and Waterproofing in Mooresville, NC

    Wet baseboards outside the shower or a stain on the ceiling below usually point to the pan. We plug the drain, fill the pan with an inch or two of water, and mark the waterline and check it a day later. A drop means a leak. Rebuilding means a mortar pre-slope of a quarter inch per foot, a new PVC liner or bonded membrane, and a two-piece clamping drain set on the liner. We flood test the new pan before any tile goes down.
