    What Goes Into a Repipe in Wilmington, NC

    PEX Pipe Replacement in Wilmington

    PEX bends around corners and comes in long rolls, so a typical repipe needs fewer fittings than copper would on the same layout. Disconnecting garden hoses and insulating exposed lines are easy steps that help homes around Wilmington get through a cold night. It often survives freezing where copper would crack. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings where possible, which give a full-bore connection and hold up well over time. Faster install.

    Emergency Pipe Replacement in Wilmington, NC

    A burst pipe needs fast action. Shut off the main valve first. Then call, because water spreading through walls and floors does damage every minute, and we can usually get there, stop the leak and replace the failed section the same day. Drying comes next.

    Pipe Replacement for Multi-Unit Buildings in Wilmington

    Apartments and condos share risers. One leak can affect several units. We plan riser replacement in phases, one stack at a time, keeping each unit informed. Organized work.

    Polybutylene Pipe Replacement in Wilmington

    Polybutylene is gray or blue plastic installed from roughly the late 1970s to mid 1990s. Chlorine breaks it down over time. Failures tend to be sudden splits at fittings rather than slow drips, and some insurers refuse to cover homes that still have it. Replace it.

    PEX Repiping in Wilmington

    PEX installs quicker. Flexible lines snake through walls with fewer openings, while manifold setups give you separate shutoffs. Popular choice.

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Wilmington, NC

    We often leave old galvanized pipe in the wall. It is disconnected and capped, while new lines run beside it, so we avoid tearing open walls just to pull out dead pipe. Only accessible pipe comes out.

    Slab Leak Pipe Repair and Replacement in Wilmington

    Water under a slab shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a sound of running water, or a meter that spins with everything off. Finding the exact spot comes first. Then you have a choice between breaking the slab to fix that spot or rerouting the line through walls or the attic, which skips the jackhammer. We explain both.
