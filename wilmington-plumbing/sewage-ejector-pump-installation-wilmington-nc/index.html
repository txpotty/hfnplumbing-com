    Ejector Pump Replacement in Wilmington

    Ejector pumps usually last 7 to 10 years, depending on how often they cycle and what gets flushed. Watch for constant running, a hum or grind, repeated alarms and odor at the pit, since any of them means trouble is close. Old switches fail first.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in Wilmington

    Digging the pit starts with a saw cut through the floor, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor. Dense red clay is common in central parts of North Carolina, and it holds water and shifts with the seasons, stressing buried pipes. We bed the basin on gravel and backfill around it so it cannot float when groundwater rises, then pour new concrete to seal the floor and lock the basin in place. Floating basins crack floors.

    Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in Wilmington

    A high water alarm belongs on every ejector system, since a pump failure can lead to a sewage backup quickly. Audible alarms on their own power or Wi-Fi alarms that text you give warning while the pit is still filling, not after waste reaches the floor. Flooding can back up municipal sewers and saturate septic drain fields, causing slow drains and backups inside the home. Battery backup is an option.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin Installation in Wilmington, NC

    The basin is where the system lives, and a 30-gallon size is common for a single basement bathroom. Bigger basins let the pump run longer, fewer times a day, which is easier on the motor and switch, so we size up when the pit serves a laundry or several fixtures. Lids must be gas-tight.

    Cost-Effective Sewage Ejector Pump Upgrades in Wilmington, NC

    Upgrading an old ejector does not always mean a new basin. If the basin is sound, we can swap in a better pump, replace the old swing check with a new one, add a full-port shutoff and install a high water alarm, which covers most of what a new system offers for a fraction of the cost. We tell you if the basin is done.
