    Reduced Pressure Zone Assembly Service in Burlington, NC

    RPZ assemblies protect against high-hazard cross-connections at restaurants, medical offices, car washes and buildings with chemical feed. Two independent checks sit on either side of a relief valve, which opens and discharges water when a check fails. That discharge needs a drain. RPZs from all the major makers are on our list.

    Febco Backflow Preventer Experts in Burlington

    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer in Burlington

    A standard backflow test in Burlington, NC usually runs $75 to $150. Rebuilds tend to fall between $200 and $600 depending on which parts fail, while a new assembly can run $450 to $1,500 or more on a larger commercial line. Pipe size matters most. You get a quote before any work starts.

    A standard test usually takes 20 to 45 minutes per assembly. Water is briefly shut off during the test, so we let you know before we close the valve on the supply side. A rebuild adds about an hour.

    An RPZ has two checks with a relief valve between them, and it is rated for high-hazard lines. A double check has the two checks but no relief valve, which limits it to lower hazards such as many fire and irrigation lines. Code decides which one.

    A brass assembly often lasts 10 to 20 years, sometimes longer. The rubber parts inside wear out much sooner, which is why rebuild kits exist and why most failed tests are fixed without replacing the body. Cold snaps are hard on them.

    In most cases, yes, installing or replacing an assembly in Burlington needs a plumbing permit from the local building department. An inspector checks that the device matches the hazard, sits at the right height and has room for testing and drainage. Permits are on us.

    Water dripping or running from the relief valve on an RPZ is the clearest sign. Low pressure at the house, a cracked bonnet after a freeze or water that tastes or smells off can also point to the assembly. Have it checked promptly.

    Yearly testing is the norm for water utilities. That schedule exists because rubber parts wear and debris settles on the seats, and neither one is visible from outside the valve. We send reminders.
