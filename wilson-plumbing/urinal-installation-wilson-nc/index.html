    Residential Urinal Plumbing in Wilson, NC

    Home urinals use the same rules as commercial ones. Expect a 2 inch drain, a built-in trap, a vent and a supply sized to the flush valve. Your city may want a permit. We check that first.

    ADA Urinal Installation in Wilson

    Accessible urinals start at the carrier. We set the carrier so the finished rim lands at 17 inches or lower, keep the clear floor space free of partitions and door swings, and mount the flush handle or sensor where a seated user can reach it. Measurements get checked twice. Inspectors measure too.

    Old Urinal Removal and Replacement in Wilson

    Old urinals come off harder than they went on. Rusted bolts and seized valves often have to be cut, so we protect the tile while working. We never reuse the old gasket. The flange gets cleaned to bare metal.

    Office and Restaurant Urinal Installation in Wilson

    Restaurant restrooms see a lot of traffic in short bursts. A single urinal at a busy bar can flush a few hundred times on a Friday night, so we install fixtures and valves that recover quickly and stand up to rough handling without loosening on the wall. Vandal-resistant valves are available. Night installs keep you open.

    Sensor Urinal Installation in Wilson, NC

    Sensor valves stop missed flushes and double flushes. We install battery or hardwired units, set the sensing range so the valve does not fire when someone walks past, and turn on a sanitary flush cycle if the restroom sits empty for long stretches. Expect years between battery changes. Hardwired units need an electrician first.

    ADA-Compliant Urinal Installation in Wilson

    At least one urinal in a public restroom with more than one usually has to be accessible. That means a rim no higher than 17 inches above the floor, an elongated bowl projecting at least 13-1/2 inches from the wall, and a 30 by 48 inch clear floor space in front. Flush controls must be reachable. We mark it all during rough-in.

    Residential Urinal Installation in Wilson, NC

    Basement and garage bathrooms are where most home urinals go. We tie into the existing drain and vent with a 2 inch line, run a 3/4 or 1/2 inch supply depending on the flush valve, and frame in blocking or a compact carrier behind the wall. Ultra-low-flow models are popular. That barely touches the water bill.

    Waterless Urinal Installation in Wilson

    Waterless urinals save the most water and need the most discipline. They use a cartridge or a layer of sealing liquid that lets urine pass while blocking sewer gas, and the cartridge has to be swapped on schedule or the restroom starts to smell. Cleaning staff need a quick lesson. We show them how.
