    Gas Appliance Connector Repair in Kannapolis, NC

    Weak flames or a smell near one appliance often point to the connector or shutoff behind it. Uncoated brass connectors from decades ago crack at the flare fittings and should be replaced on sight. We replace them with coated stainless connectors. We also add a reachable shutoff and a drip leg where needed, and confirm the new connector is rated for that appliance's BTU input.

    Gas Shut Off by the Utility in Kannapolis

    When your utility shuts off gas for a leak, you may be without hot water, heat or cooking until a licensed plumber fixes it. Our plumber finds and fixes the leak, then runs the test your gas company requires before unlocking the meter. The truck carries what we need. We do not leave until the gauge holds steady and the system is ready for the utility.

    Pool Heater, Fire Pit and Grill Lines in Kannapolis, NC

    Backyard gas lines for pool heaters, fire pits and built-in grills face sun, weather and irrigation water that shorten their life. A pool heater can pull 250,000 BTU or more, which can rob gas from the furnace and water heater if the line was sized for a grill. We size outdoor lines for the full load. New and repaired lines get tested, buried to the required depth and laid with tracer wire.

    Interior Black Iron Pipe Repair in Kannapolis

    Threaded black iron pipe can leak at joints after years of vibration or settling. We tighten, reseal or replace fittings and sections with new pipe and gas-rated thread sealant. Rusted pipe goes too. Pipe in a garage, crawlspace or exterior wall that shows heavy rust gets replaced before it turns into a leak, and the whole system gets a pressure test after the repair.

    Gas Leak Detection in Kannapolis

    A rotten egg smell, hissing near a line, dead grass in a stripe across the yard or a gas meter that moves with everything off are the common leak signs. Trust your nose. Our plumbers isolate the system section by section, sweep it with an electronic detector and confirm the exact spot with soap solution. Once we find it, you get a written repair price before work starts.

    Emergency Gas Line Repair in Kannapolis

    If you smell gas, leave the building, skip the light switches and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Then call us. Once the utility has shut off the meter and made the area safe, we locate the leak, make the repair and run the pressure test needed before service can be restored. Pilots get relit once gas is back. Most emergency repairs are done in a single visit with parts from the truck.

    After-Hours Gas Line Repair in Kannapolis

    Gas problems do not wait for business hours, and losing heat or hot water overnight is a real problem. We answer late calls. Once everyone is out and the gas company has secured things, we send a plumber to find the leak, fix it and test the system. We coordinate with the utility on the meter.

    Natural Gas Leak Detection and Repair in Kannapolis

    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Repair in Kannapolis

    Small fitting or connector repairs typically cost $150 to $450. Section replacements run $400 to $1,200, and buried line replacement can reach $1,000 to $4,000 or more.

    We work on black iron, coated steel, CSST such as Gastite and TracPipe, and polyethylene for buried lines. Each material has its place. PE stays underground with an approved riser at the house, CSST requires bonding, and black iron needs supports and the right thread compound.

    Most standard repairs, like fixing a small leak or replacing a short section, are done in a few hours. Relocations take more time. A long replacement or a reroute can take a day or need a second visit, and we tell you the expected timeline after the inspection.

    Not safe at all. Gas line repair should be done by a licensed plumber with a detector and a test gauge. Bad repairs can cause leaks, fires or CO problems, and they leave no permit record for insurers or future buyers.

    Utilities lock the meter when they find a leak or a failed test. Service stays off until a licensed plumber fixes it and the system passes a test.

    Leaks at the meter or on the utility's service line belong to the gas company. Leave the meter alone. Get out of the house, call the utility's emergency line, and once they have handled their side, we can repair any leaks on the house side of the meter, including the riser and the yard line.

    There is no required yearly inspection for most homes. A look every few years helps older houses. Old brass connectors, decades-old buried steel and appliances with yellow or weak flames are all good reasons to have the system checked.

    Tightening a union or swapping a connector takes an hour or two. Buried lines take longer. Replacing a long section of buried line usually takes most of a day for digging, laying pipe, testing and backfill, plus whatever time the inspection needs.
