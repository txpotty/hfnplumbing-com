    Tankless Error Code Diagnostics in Kannapolis, NC

    Every tankless brand flashes its own codes, and the same number can mean different things on two units.     Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Repair in Kannapolis, NC

    Rinnai units are common, and most of their trouble starts with scale. The LC code is the unit's own warning that the exchanger needs a flush.     Igniter and Flame Rod Repair in Kannapolis, NC

    Gas tankless heaters light with a hot surface or spark igniter, then prove flame with a flame rod. An oxidized flame rod gives a weak microamp reading and the board shuts the gas valve after a few seconds. We clean or replace the rod, check the igniter for cracks or weak spark, and confirm the gas valve opens on call. Five-minute fix sometimes.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Repair in Kannapolis

    Navien condensing units use a secondary stainless exchanger to recover heat from the flue gas. That makes condensate, and a plugged trap or a full neutralizer will shut the heater down with a drain or flue error. We clear the trap, check the PVC vent pitch and test the circulation pump on NPE-A models with built-in recirculation. Then we fire it at low and high flow.

    Ignition Failure Repair in Kannapolis, NC

    No ignition codes have four usual causes: a dirty flame rod, a bad igniter, low gas pressure or a blocked intake. Undersized gas pipe is the one people miss. A tankless burner can need 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, and the half-inch line that fed a 40,000 BTU tank often cannot carry that, which is why it fires at low flow and quits when two showers are on.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Repair in Kannapolis

    Minor fixes like a flame rod or a flow sensor usually run $150 to $350. A heat exchanger or control board out of warranty can push a repair past $800, and at that point we price a replacement beside it. You see the number first.

    Low gas pressure under full load is the most common reason, followed by a dirty flame rod or a blocked intake. Scale that makes the exchanger overheat can do it too. We test each one in turn.

    Cleaning the inlet filter and wiping down the intake screen are fair homeowner jobs.     In Kannapolis, swapping a sensor or flame rod usually does not need a permit. Changes to the gas line, venting or the unit itself typically need one, and we handle that filing.

    Descale yearly on hard water and clean the inlet screen two or three times a year. A softener or scale-reduction cartridge ahead of the heater slows scale a lot, and keeping the intake clear of leaves and lint heads off combustion faults. Check the vent cap after storms.
