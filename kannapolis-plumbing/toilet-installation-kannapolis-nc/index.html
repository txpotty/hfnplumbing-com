    ADA-Compliant Toilet Installation in Kannapolis

    ADA toilets put the seat 17 to 19 inches off the floor, and the centerline sits 16 to 18 inches from the side wall. It is set that way so a grab bar fits. Before anything is ordered we measure the drain, because shifting it to meet the ADA centerline means cutting into the floor. Elongated bowls are standard here.

    Toilet Brands We Install in Kannapolis

    We install Kohler, TOTO, American Standard, Gerber and most other brands sold at supply houses and home centers. Each one has its own parts quirks. Some skirted TOTO models use a Unifit adapter, and certain Kohler tanks take a gasket that hardware stores rarely stock. We keep those parts on hand.

    Comfort Height Toilet Installation in Kannapolis, NC

    Comfort height bowls stand about two inches taller than standard ones. Think about who will use it before you buy. Shorter adults and kids sometimes find the taller seat leaves their feet dangling, while anyone with bad knees or a hip replacement usually finds it much easier to sit and stand. Installation is the same as a standard bowl.

    Wall-Hung Carrier Systems in Kannapolis

    In-wall carriers from Geberit and Grohe each mount a little differently, and the instructions matter. We follow the maker's frame height chart exactly. A frame set wrong puts the bowl too low or too high, and once tile is up there is no adjusting it. Measure twice here.

    Bathroom Remodel Toilet Installation in Kannapolis, NC

    New tile over old vinyl can raise a bathroom floor by half an inch or more. Suddenly the old flange is below the floor. We bring the flange up with a stacking extender or replace it outright, and if you are moving the toilet on a slab, that means cutting concrete to reroute the drain and patching it afterward. Moving it on a wood floor is easier.

    Rough-In Toilet Installation in Kannapolis, NC

    Rough-in is the plumbing that goes in before walls and floors close up. For a toilet that means a 3 inch drain with a vent, plus a cold supply stub. We set the drain 12 inches from the framed wall plus the thickness of drywall, so the finished rough-in lands right where the fixture expects it. Then the inspector sees it open.

    Smart Toilet Installation in Kannapolis, NC

    Smart toilets with heated seats and built-in bidets need two things the old toilet did not: power and a dedicated water connection. Most need a GFCI outlet close enough for the cord. We run the water side and mount the unit, and if a new outlet is needed we bring in a licensed electrician rather than splicing anything ourselves. Then we test every button.

    Smart Toilet and Bidet Seat Installation in Kannapolis

    Bidet seats like the TOTO Washlet and Bio Bidet bolt onto most existing elongated bowls. A T-valve splits water off the fill line. Heated seats with a warm-air dryer need a nearby GFCI outlet, and when there is none we bring in an electrician first. Non-electric seats take about thirty minutes.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Kannapolis

    Labor for a standard toilet installation in Kannapolis typically falls between $250 and $600, not counting the toilet itself. Flange repairs add to that. A rotted flange or a soft subfloor can push the total higher, and we price that work before it starts instead of surprising you on the invoice.

    Watch the base and the tank. Water that reappears on the floor after you wipe it up points to a cracked bowl or a failed seal. We rule out the seal first because it is cheaper, but cracked porcelain cannot be fixed for good, so a crack means a new toilet.

    Municipal water in North Carolina is generally soft, but many private wells produce acidic water that can corrode copper pipe over time. Hard water leaves scale in the rim jets and on the fill valve over time. Weak flushes follow. A toilet with a well-glazed trapway and an easy-to-reach fill valve holds up better, and a cup of vinegar left in the overflow tube overnight now and then keeps the rim jets open.

    It is a toilet with the seat about 17 to 19 inches off the floor, roughly chair height, compared with 15 inches or so on a standard bowl. Knees and hips usually like it. Children and shorter people may find their feet dangle, so for a family bath a standard height with a grab bar can be the better call.

    Adding or moving a toilet almost always needs one. Swapping in the same place usually does not. An inspector checks the new drain and vent while things are still open, and we book that visit around your remodel schedule.

    About two hours for most floor-mount toilets. Smart and wall-mounted units run longer. Wall-mounted units usually need two visits, carrier first and bowl after the finish wall, and a smart toilet can hinge on the electrician.

    Homeowners set basic toilets all the time. The trouble spots are flange height and bolt torque. A flange that sits below the finished floor needs an extender or a thicker seal, and a nut tightened one turn too far can crack the base, so on new floors and wall-hung toilets a pro install is the safer bet.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Twelve inches is the most common.
