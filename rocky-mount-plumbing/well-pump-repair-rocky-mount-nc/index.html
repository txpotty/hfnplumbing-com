    Jet Pump Repair in Rocky Mount, NC

    Shallow wells and booster systems often use jet pumps. Common problems are loss of prime, a leaking shaft seal and a worn impeller. Our crew in Rocky Mount restores prime, replaces worn parts and checks the suction line for air leaks. That matters most where water levels rise and fall through the year, since a dropping level can starve the pump and cost it prime.

    Control Box and Surge Repair in Rocky Mount, NC

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. Flooding can overwhelm municipal sewers and saturate septic drain fields, causing slow drains and backups inside the home. Lightning and power surges damage those parts more than anything else. After testing the capacitor, relay and overload, we replace the bad part and suggest a surge arrestor if the box lacks one. An arrestor costs little.

    Low Water Pressure Diagnosis in Rocky Mount

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. A clogged sediment filter, a pressure switch set too low or a leak in the yard line can all cause it. In some cases the pump is fine and the well just cannot keep up with demand. We test each one.

    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in Rocky Mount

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Sensor failures and drive errors are the usual problems. HFN Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Rocky Mount, NC well. Electronics fail less often than people think.

    Constant Pressure System Repair in Rocky Mount, NC

    Variable speed drives like the Grundfos SQE and Franklin SubDrive keep pressure steady by changing motor speed. A fault code on the drive points us in the right direction. The drive, sensor and wiring get tested, and the failed part gets repaired or replaced before we verify the setpoint. These need a smaller tank.

    Well Pump Maintenance and Repair Cost in Rocky Mount, NC

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Rocky Mount

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. The usual signs are no water at all, weak pressure or a hum with no flow. We test the motor from the surface first. If it has to come up, a pump hoist lifts the pipe safely, and we replace the burned motor, worn impellers or a bad check valve before setting it back.
