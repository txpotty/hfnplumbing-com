    House-to-Tank Line Repair in Concord

    Gurgling drains right after a pump-out point to the line between the house and the tank, not the tank itself. Roots and settling ground cause most sags and breaks in that run. A camera from the house cleanout finds the bad joint, and we mark it in the yard. Then we dig one hole, not a trench. A second cleanout near the tank goes in while it is open.

    Septic Repair Costs Compared in Concord, NC

    Baffle or tee replacement often runs $300 to $900. Replacing an effluent pump, floats included, typically costs $900 to $2,000, and fixing the house-to-tank line costs $800 to $3,000 based on how deep and long it is. Leach field restoration or replacement can cost $5,000 to $20,000 or more. The cheapest fix that actually works is the one we push for.

    Septic Lid and Riser Repair in Concord

    Old concrete lids crack, and a cracked lid over a 1,000-gallon tank is a real danger to kids and pets. We replace failing lids and add risers that bring access up to the lawn. Future pumping gets cheaper. Each lid we install gets screws or a lock so a child cannot lift it.

    Inlet and Outlet Baffle Replacement in Concord, NC

    Baffles hold back the scum layer on top and the sludge on the bottom so only clear effluent leaves the tank. Concrete ones corrode from the acid that sewer gas forms and eventually fall off into the tank. With no outlet baffle, sludge flows right out to the leach lines. We replace them with PVC sanitary tees. Usually a short job.

    Drain Field Line Jetting in Concord

    Distribution laterals clog from the inside with solids and slime, especially after years without pumping. A jetter goes into each lateral from the D-box or an end cleanout, and the water that comes back tells us how bad it is. Roots get cut. If the soil itself has sealed, jetting will not fix it, and we say so.

    Septic Maintenance and Small Repairs in Concord

    A sludge judge, a long clear tube, tells us how full the tank really is. Each maintenance call includes a sludge and scum reading, and pumping is due once solids fill roughly a third of the tank. Filters get rinsed. Small cracks around the riser get sealed before groundwater finds them.
