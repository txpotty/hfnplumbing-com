    Water Heater Inspections in Concord

    Water heaters give clues long before they fail. The serial number tells us the age, and we check for rust, look at the vent and test the relief valve. Tankless units get checked for scale.     Leak Detection and Slab Screening in Concord

    A spinning water meter with all fixtures off is a clear sign of a hidden leak. We also check for warm floor spots. Meter tests, pressure loss checks and a look for damp baseboards come first, and slab homes get acoustic and thermal follow-up. Catching a slab leak early limits damage to floors and foundations.

    Sewer Line Camera Inspections in Concord, NC

    A sewer camera shows the inside of the main drain without digging.     Sewer Line Camera Inspection in Concord, NC

    Older homes in Concord often have cast iron or clay sewer pipe that cracks, scales up or lets roots in at the joints.     Preventive Maintenance Checks in Concord, NC

    A maintenance check is a lighter version of a full inspection for owners who want to stay ahead of problems. We flush the water heater, exercise the main shutoff and angle stops, check pressure and look for slow drains and weeping fittings. It takes about an hour. Doing this every year or two adds years to a water heater and keeps the shutoffs working for the day you really need them.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in Concord

    A standard plumbing inspection in Concord, NC usually costs $150 to $350. Size changes the price. The base price covers pipe, fixtures, the heater, drains and pressure, and the sewer camera is usually a separate line since it adds time and equipment. You get the price when you book.

    Looking for drips and watching the meter is worth doing on your own. It just goes only so far. A professional inspection uses cameras, pressure gauges and leak detection tools to check the parts you cannot see, including the sewer line and the pipe inside walls, which is where the expensive problems usually hide.

    Watching your water meter, looking under sinks and checking for leaks are all things you can do yourself. A plumber adds video, pressure tests and an expert read.

    Watch for a water bill that jumps for no reason, low pressure, slow drains or brown water. Gurgling drains count too. Damp spots on walls or floors, a musty smell under a sink or a warm patch of tile can all point to a hidden leak that an inspection will find before it does more damage.

    A standard home inspection takes about one and a half to three hours. Sewer video adds time. Big houses, long sewers or a leak that needs tracking can stretch it, and we give you a time estimate when you schedule.

    Our plumber explains each issue, how serious it is and what the repair options are. You decide what happens next. We can quote any repairs on the spot or later, and you can have us do the work, use another plumber or hand the report to the seller during negotiations.

    Plan on one to two hours for most residential inspections. Big homes and long sewers add time.
