    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Concord

    Figure on roughly $800 to $2,000 for most basement sump pump installs in Concord. The low end covers a pump swap in an existing pit, while a new pit cut through the slab, a long discharge run, or a battery backup pushes the total toward the top of that range. You get a written price after we look.

    Discharge belongs in the yard, a dry well, or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Most local codes do not allow sump water in the sanitary sewer, and it can draw a fine.

    Quality cast iron pumps usually carry three to five years of factory coverage. Our labor has its own warranty on top of that, and we walk you through the exact terms for the model you pick before we install it.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. What decides it is how fast the pit fills and how high the water rises, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Oversizing causes short cycling.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and a concrete patch takes most of a day, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Local inspectors review permitted work, and HFN Plumbing handles the permit and inspection steps so the job is approved the first time. New pits, new circuits and new discharge routes are the parts most likely to need a plumbing permit. We check the local rules before the job starts, and when a permit applies we pull it and schedule the inspection ourselves. No trips to the permit office.

    A primary pump does the everyday work. A secondary pump, often on a battery, waits in the same pit, and it kicks on if the primary fails, loses power, or gets overwhelmed by more water than it can move during a heavy storm. Two pumps, two check valves.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Rust on the housing and constant on-off cycling are others, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. Past ten years, replace it.
