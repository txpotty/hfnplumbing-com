    Roots grow toward the water and nutrients inside sewer pipe. A hairline crack is enough. Once a root tip gets in through a cracked joint or loose seal, it grows into a mass that catches paper and grease, and clay and older concrete pipe with many joints are the most vulnerable.

    Clearing a clog does not need a permit. Pipe repairs often require one. Sewer section replacements and new cleanouts generally need a permit, which we handle.

    Store-bought cleaners rarely clear a real clog. They often make it worse. The chemicals sit in the trap, heat up and can damage older pipe or soften glue joints, and if the clog does not clear, the plumber now has to open a trap full of caustic liquid. A cable is safer.

    Mineral scale inside pipes narrows them and traps grease, hair and soap.     Slow sinks and gurgling drains are the early signs. A sewer smell in the yard or basement comes next. In the yard, standing water a day after rain or washed-out mulch means the water has nowhere to go.
