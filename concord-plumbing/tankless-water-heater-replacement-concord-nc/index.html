    Exterior Mount Tankless Units in Concord, NC

    Exterior-mount tankless units hang on an outside wall and vent straight out the front, so there is no vent pipe to run. Covers and wiring take a beating outdoors. Hard freezes are uncommon along the coast but happen most winters in the mountains, where pipes in crawlspaces and outside walls need protection. We check the freeze protection settings and the pipe cover kit on every outdoor swap. Often the simplest job we do.

    Commercial Tankless Replacement in Concord

    Cascade controls on a commercial rack decide which unit fires first, and after one unit is replaced they need rebalancing. Otherwise one unit does all the work. Descaling gets scheduled by usage.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Concord

    Rinnai units we pull are usually 12 to 18 years old, and the failure tends to be a leaking heat exchanger or a dead control board. Newer Rinnai models usually fit the old mounting pattern. Less drywall work that way. Venting stays the same, or switches to PVC if you move to a condensing unit.

    Point-of-Use Electric Tankless in Concord

    Point-of-use electric tankless heaters under a sink are a different animal from whole-house units. They run on a single 20 or 30 amp circuit and work well for a far bathroom or an office sink. Most take under two hours.

    Whole-House Electric Tankless Replacement in Concord

    Whole-house electric tankless performance drops when the incoming water is cold, since the unit has to raise the temperature further at the same amperage. Stiebel Eltron and EcoSmart are the common brands. A heat pump water heater is sometimes the better fit, and we will tell you.

    Commercial Tankless Racks in Concord, NC

    Restaurants and salons run tankless racks hard. Four units together can pull over 600,000 BTU, which means checking gas pressure with all of them running. Installs can happen after closing.

    Rinnai Condensing Upgrades in Concord, NC

    Rinnai Sensei condensing models replace older non-condensing Rinnai units in a lot of homes, but the venting changes from stainless to PVC. We handle that conversion. Gas and water connections usually line up.

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Concord, NC

    Pulling a 50 gallon tank out of a garage frees up floor space and ends standby heat loss. The catch is gas, since most tankless units need a 3/4-inch line or bigger plus new venting. Before quoting, we check the meter and pipe run, and afterward we cap the old flue and seal the roof. Plan on a full day.
