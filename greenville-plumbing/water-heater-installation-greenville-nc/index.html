    Expansion Tank Installation in Greenville, NC

    A pressure reducing valve or check valve on the main turns your plumbing into a closed system. When the water heats and expands with nowhere to go, pressure climbs and stresses the relief valve, the toilet fill valves and every supply line in the house. The tank soaks up that extra volume. We pre-charge it to match your water pressure.

    Gas Water Heater Replacement in Greenville

    Swapping a gas water heater comes down to three connections done right, the gas, the vent and the water. Each gas swap gets a new connector, a drip leg, a draft test at the hood and a leak check with a detector at every fitting. Venting is what keeps CO out. All work follows local code.

    Water Heater Expansion Tank Installation in Greenville

    Closed plumbing systems trap the extra volume water gains when it heats. Permits are typically required for water heater replacements, new fixtures, sewer line work and remodels that relocate plumbing. An expansion tank gives that water somewhere to go, so the relief valve stops dripping and pipe joints stop taking pressure spikes every time the burner runs. We pre-charge it to your house pressure. It mounts on the cold inlet.

    Brand-Specific Water Heater Installation in Greenville

    Each manufacturer publishes its own install rules, and they are not identical. Clearances, maximum vent lengths and relief valve rules differ by brand, and warranty claims get denied when an installer skipped them. We follow them exactly. Your warranty stays intact.

    Traditional Tank Water Heater Installation in Greenville

    Tank heaters still heat most homes' water, holding 40 to 75 gallons at temperature until you need it.     Electric Water Heater Installation in Greenville, NC

    Electric tanks need a 240-volt circuit, usually a 30-amp double-pole breaker with 10-gauge wire. Before hooking up the new tank we check that the breaker and wire size match the elements, since a mismatch is a common cause of tripped breakers and scorched terminals. No venting needed. You get more spots to put it.
