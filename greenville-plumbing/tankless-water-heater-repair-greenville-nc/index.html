    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Repair in Greenville, NC

    Tankless Water Heater Descaling Service in Greenville

    Scale is calcium and magnesium carbonate that drops out of hard water when it heats, and a tankless exchanger heats water fast through narrow passages. Well owners around Greenville benefit from periodic water testing and, when needed, neutralizers or filters that protect pipes and appliances. Our tech connects a pump to the isolation valves and runs descaler through the exchanger for close to an hour. Then we flush with clean water. The inlet screen gets cleaned too.

    Noritz Tankless Water Heater Repair in Greenville, NC

    Noritz heaters hold up well, but their flow sensors and vent connections still wear out. A code 90 points to combustion trouble, often a dirty fan or a restricted intake. We clean the fan and burner, check the vent joints for gaps, and test the flow sensor's output against the spec sheet. Gaskets get replaced if they leak.

    Ignition Failure Repair in Greenville, NC

    No ignition codes have four usual causes: a dirty flame rod, a bad igniter, low gas pressure or a blocked intake. Undersized gas pipe is the one people miss. A tankless burner can need 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, and the half-inch line that fed a 40,000 BTU tank often cannot carry that, which is why it fires at low flow and quits when two showers are on.

    Flow Sensor and Thermistor Repair in Greenville

    Tankless heaters start when the flow sensor sees about half a gallon a minute, and they hold temperature off thermistor readings. A dirty inlet screen or a failing sensor shows up as no hot water, temperature swings or a unit that drops out mid-shower. We pull the screen, read sensor values in the service menu and compare thermistor resistance to the chart. Usually under an hour.

    Navien Condensate & Fan Repair in Greenville

    Navien's E016 is a heat exchanger overheat, and scale or a backed-up condensate line is usually behind it. We also read fan speed from the service menu and compare it to spec, since a fan that cannot reach target RPM will cause flame and exhaust faults on the same unit. Clean trap, clean screen, retest.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Repair in Greenville

    Minor fixes like a flame rod or a flow sensor tend to land between $150 and $350. A heat exchanger or control board out of warranty can push a repair past $800, and at that point we price a replacement beside it. You see the number first.

    Scale is first by a wide margin. After that come dirty flame rods, failed flow sensors and blocked air intakes, with control boards and fan motors showing up less often, usually on units past ten years old. Most of it is preventable.

    Watch for hot water that takes longer to show up, a burner that cycles on and off, water around the base, or a code on the display. A drip inside the cabinet rusts parts fast.

    Codes fire when a sensor reads something outside its range: low flow, no flame signal, high exhaust temperature or a blocked vent. Each one maps to a specific test.

    Low flow at a single fixture, a dirty inlet filter, a failing flow sensor or scale will all cause temperature swings. A crossed hot and cold line at a single-handle faucet can do it too, and that one is not the heater at all.
