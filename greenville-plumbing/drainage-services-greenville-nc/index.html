    Frequently Asked Questions About Drainage Services in Greenville

    Most basic drain clearing in Greenville, NC runs $150 to $500. Main lines cost more than sinks. Hydro jetting runs higher than cabling because of the equipment and time, and sewer line repair can range from about $1,500 for a spot repair to well over $8,000 for a full replacement with digging.

    Roots usually enter through an existing crack or joint, then widen it as they grow. Over years, that can split clay pipe. Roots can offset joints and, in bad cases, collapse the pipe, which then needs digging or lining.

    Clearing a clog does not need a permit. Pipe repairs often require one. Replacing a section of sewer, adding a cleanout, or connecting a new drain to the sewer generally requires a permit and an inspection, and we handle that paperwork and schedule the inspector as part of the job.

    Slow sinks and gurgling drains are the early signs. Sewer odor near a drain or cleanout often follows. Outside, look for water that stands more than a day after rain, soggy spots along the foundation, or mulch and soil washed onto the walk, all of which mean water is not leaving the yard the way it should.

    We install and service Zoeller, Little Giant, Wayne and Liberty pumps. Cast iron models last longest. Battery backups with their own float and an alarm cover power outages and pump failures.

    Drain cleaning usually means a cable machine with a cutting head that bores through the clog. It works fast and costs less. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the whole pipe wall clean of grease, scale and roots, so it lasts longer but costs more and needs a pipe that is in good enough shape to take the pressure.

    Snaking is the right first step for most single clogs. It is fast and inexpensive. Jetting is better for grease-coated kitchen lines, scaled cast iron and roots that keep coming back, because it cleans the pipe wall instead of punching a hole through the blockage.

    Call when more than one drain is slow, or when a toilet gurgles while the washer drains. That usually means the main line. In the yard, water that stands for a day or more after rain is the cue.
