    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Greenville

    Small under-sink traps often start around $200 a visit. Large outdoor interceptors can pass $1,000 when they are full and the cap has hardened. The biggest variable is the gallons hauled, because disposal facilities charge by volume and a 1,500-gallon tank is a full truckload.

    Slow sinks come first. Then gurgling floor drains and a sour smell near the dish area. Gray water rising through a floor sink means the trap is far past 25 percent and grease is already moving downstream.

    Everything in the trap comes out, from the floating grease cap down to the sludge of food solids on the floor. The industry calls it brown grease. Yellow grease, the used fryer oil in your collection bin, is a separate product that gets recycled and should never go into the trap.

    Grease passes the outlet and plugs the line. Then the backups start, and they tend to hit during the rush. Beyond the mess, many sewer districts fine kitchens for grease discharges and can bill them for jetting the public main, and a health inspector can close a kitchen with sewage on the floor.

    It goes to a permitted receiving facility. Some facilities process it for biofuel or digesters. What is left gets dewatered and disposed of under the facility's permit, and each load is logged from your trap to their gate.

    There is no parts warranty on a pump-out, since nothing gets installed. The work itself is guaranteed. Should it drain poorly after our visit, or a backup trace back to something we missed, the crew returns and makes it right.

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps and in-ground gravity interceptors make up most of our work. Automatic grease removal units are on the list too. An AGRU uses a heater and skimmer wheel to move grease into a bin, and the bin, wheel and strainer all need regular cleaning.

    Two things, a full pump-out and a trap that drains properly when we leave. Skim-and-go is not how we work. Should a problem tied to our work show up later, the crew returns to fix it, and the depth readings on the sheet show how we left the trap.
