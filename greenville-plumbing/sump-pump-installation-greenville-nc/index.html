    High Water Alarms and Smart Monitoring in Greenville

    A high water alarm is a float switch that sounds when the pit rises past normal. Wi-Fi models send a text too. Landlords like them. They can go on a new pump or one that is already working.

    Combination Sump Pump System Installation in Greenville, NC

    Two pumps on one riser cover both kinds of failure, a dead motor and a dead outlet. The primary runs on house power and handles the everyday inflow, while the battery unit waits above it on a separate float. People with carpet downstairs buy these. They are worth it anywhere a few inches of water would mean new drywall.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Greenville, NC

    Pedestal pumps put the motor on a column above the pit, so the motor never sits in water. You can reach the motor and the switch without fishing anything out of the pit, which helps in a basement where the pit is shallow or tight. Expect more motor noise than a submersible. We set the float rod and the discharge so the pump cycles cleanly.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Greenville, NC

    Backup systems we install use sealed AGM batteries that do not need water. The charger keeps the battery topped off. Batteries need replacing every few years. They matter most below finished rooms.

    Combination Sump Pump Systems in Greenville

    Combination units pair a primary pump and a battery backup on one pre-plumbed discharge assembly. They fit pits where two separate pumps would crowd each other. That saves some labor. We set them in homes that want two layers without a bigger pit.

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Greenville

    Submersible pumps live at the bottom of the pit with a sealed, oil-cooled motor. They run quieter and move more water. Lids close flat over them. We seal the cord grommet and the lid on every install.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Installation in Greenville

    Most Greenville, NC sump pump jobs land between $600 and $1,800. That range covers a quality pump, a new check valve, the discharge fittings and the labor to set and test it in a pit that already exists. New pits cost more. Every line item is on the quote.

    Manufacturer warranties usually run three to five years, longer on premium models. Our labor carries its own warranty.

    A submersible sits in the pit under water, so it runs quieter and takes no floor space. The pedestal type mounts the motor on a column above the water, which makes it easier to reach for service and usually cheaper to buy, though you will hear it. Both work. The pit and the noise you can live with decide it.

    Most standard installs take three to six hours. A new pit can stretch it to a full day. A simple swap is quicker.

    Plan on seven to ten years for a well-kept pump. A pump that cycles every few minutes through a wet spring wears out faster than one that runs a few times a month, and cheap switches fail first. We can check yours.

    Grinding, rattling or a pump that runs with no water in the pit are all warning signs. Rust on the housing is another. If the basement flooded anyway, it is time.

    Most last seven to ten years. Idle pumps fail sooner, so pour a bucket in the pit every few months.

    A straight replacement often needs no permit. New pits, electrical work or new discharge connections may need one, and we will tell you.
