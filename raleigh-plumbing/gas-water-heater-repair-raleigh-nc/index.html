    Propane Water Heater Repair in Raleigh

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. Propane units need a matching orifice and a control set near 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.

    Pilot Light and Burner Issues in Raleigh

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. No flame at all usually means a plugged pilot orifice or a bad piezo. Main burner problems show up another way. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. Each part gets tested in that sequence.

    Thermostat & Gas Valve Repair in Raleigh, NC

    Scalding water at the kitchen tap is a valve problem until proven otherwise. Sensors can drift as they age, and dials sometimes get bumped past the hot mark. Our plumbers in Raleigh, NC measure the outlet temperature at the nearest fixture, compare it to the dial and replace the gas control if it is overshooting, because water at 140 degrees can burn a child's skin in a few seconds. A setting near 120 degrees works for most households.

    Pilot Light and Ignition Service in Raleigh

    No hot water and a dark pilot window usually point to the ignition side of the heater. We pull the pilot assembly and look at the thermocouple tip, the pilot orifice and the piezo igniter wire. A thermocouple that tests weak on the millivolt meter will not hold the gas valve's magnet open, so the pilot drops out a minute after you relight it by the label. Common pilot kits ride on most of our trucks.

    Tank Leaks and Anode Rods in Raleigh

    Corrosion starts at the anode rod and works its way into the steel once the rod is spent. Municipal water in North Carolina is generally soft, but many private wells produce acidic water that can corrode copper pipe over time. The tech pulls the anode, checks what metal remains and swaps it if bare core wire shows. A fresh anode, a drained tank and a relief valve that seats properly can add years to a heater in a Raleigh home that has not started leaking from the shell. A wet bottom seam is different.
