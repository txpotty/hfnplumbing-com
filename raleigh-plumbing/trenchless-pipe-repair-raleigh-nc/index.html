    Trenchless vs Traditional Repair Cost in Raleigh, NC

    Pipe Bursting Trenchless Repair in Raleigh, NC

    Bursting works when the pipe has collapsed or is too broken to line. It can go up a size. With an entry and exit pit dug, the winch pulls the head and new HDPE through, replacing the run in a day or two in most homes. HFN Plumbing joins the new pipe by heat fusion.

    Commercial Trenchless Pipe Repair in Raleigh

    Trenchless Sewer Line Repair in Raleigh

    Tree roots, corrosion and soil movement wreck most sewer lines. Coastal areas have sandy soil and high water tables, which affect how sewer lines and septic systems perform. Small cracks get a sectional liner. Longer damage gets a full-length liner, and a fully collapsed line gets burst and replaced, all without trenching across the yard or cutting the driveway at your home.

    Cured-in-Place Pipe (CIPP) Lining in Raleigh

    A CIPP liner is a felt tube soaked in resin. It hardens inside the old pipe. We pull or invert the tube into the cleaned line, inflate it against the pipe wall and cure it with hot water, steam or UV light, which leaves a jointless pipe inside the old one. It fixes cracks, breaks and roots without digging up your Raleigh yard.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Raleigh, NC

    Main water lines can also be replaced without a trench. We burst or bore. New HDPE or PEX goes in along the old path by bursting, or along a new one by directional boring, leaving lawns and drives alone. The line is pressure tested before service is restored.

    Sectional Point Repair in Raleigh

    Sometimes only one joint or a few feet of pipe is bad. A spot liner fixes that. Guided by camera, a short liner is placed over the bad section and cured, so you pay for a few feet instead of the full line. It suits isolated damage in Raleigh laterals.

    Pipe Bursting in Raleigh, NC

    Bursting is used when the old pipe is too damaged to line or needs a larger diameter. It needs two pits. A bursting head is pulled through, fracturing the old pipe outward while a new HDPE pipe follows behind, and that HDPE is fused into one continuous length with no joints at all.
