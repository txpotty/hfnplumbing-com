    Frequently Asked Questions About Slab Leak Repair in Raleigh

    Most slab leak repairs in Raleigh, NC land between $1,000 and $5,000. Access drives the price. A direct repair in an open room sits at the low end, while a long reroute or a tunnel under a wall runs toward the top of that range or beyond it. You get a clear number first.

    Water bills that jump for no reason are often first. Warm floors come next. Other clues are running water sounds with nothing on, stained or mildewed baseboards, and a slab crack that keeps growing. Any of these in a Raleigh home is worth a test.

    Direct access repairs are often a one-day job. Bigger jobs run longer. A reroute or tunnel might take several days to a week based on length and access, and the estimate covers timing.

    Older copper and galvanized steel leak most, from corrosion and wear. PEX resists corrosion. Even so, PEX can leak from a bad fitting, high water pressure or physical damage, so we see every type fail and repair each one with matching parts. That's routine in Raleigh.

    A direct repair often wraps up in one day. Reroutes take longer. Plan on two to five days for a reroute or repipe, based on house size and how easy the attic and walls are to reach. Your Raleigh estimate lists it.

    A slab leak is in pipe running within or under the concrete foundation. You can't see it. Leaks in walls, ceilings or exposed pipe usually leave a stain or a drip you can follow, while a slab leak hides under the floor and takes locating gear to find.

    Pipe in good shape with an easy-to-reach leak is fine for a spot repair. Old pipe or a history of leaks points toward a reroute.

    High bills and the sound of running water with fixtures off are the usual first clues. Feel the tile. Wet carpet, foundation cracks and a lingering musty smell also point that way, so any of them is worth an inspection. We test Raleigh homes for all of it.
