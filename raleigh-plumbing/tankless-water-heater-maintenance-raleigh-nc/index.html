    Scale on an exchanger acts like insulation. A heavily scaled unit burns more gas per shower than a clean one, and that adds up each month. You get the maintenance price beside the likely repair cost, and the decision is yours.

    Inlet Screen and Air Filter Cleaning in Raleigh

    Grit gets in. A small screen at the cold inlet catches sand and scale flakes, especially after a main break. Units with an air intake filter collect dust and lint, which can push the burner into a combustion code. We clean both on every visit.

    Commercial Tankless Maintenance in Raleigh, NC

    Commercial kitchens and salons burn through hot water all day, so their tankless units scale up much faster than a house unit. Gas pressure gets checked with every burner lit, not at idle. Inlet screens and air filters get pulled and cleaned. Downtime is the real cost. So we schedule the work before opening or after close.

    Yearly Tankless Inspection in Raleigh

    A yearly inspection costs less than one service call for a dead heater. Gas connections get a leak test, and we work the pressure relief valve. We pull the air filter and check the igniter. Anything that is wearing out goes on a written list with a plain price, and you decide what gets done now and what can wait until next year.

    Condensate and Neutralizer Service in Raleigh

    Condensing tankless heaters drip acidic condensate, often around pH 3 to 4, and it needs to pass through a neutralizer before it reaches a drain. We clean the trap and look at the neutralizer media. We replace spent media. Where the line runs outside, we check that it is supported and protected.

    Multi-Unit Tankless Checks in Raleigh, NC

    Restaurants and salons often run two or more tankless heaters on a shared manifold, and one weak unit can drag the whole bank down. Gas pressure gets read with every unit firing together. We take a flow and temperature reading on each heater. Then we compare them.

    Tankless Vent Inspection in Raleigh

    Gas tankless heaters push exhaust out through a vent that has to stay sealed from the heater all the way to the termination outside the house. On a condensing unit the PVC or polypropylene vent should pitch back toward the heater. Every joint gets checked, and we test for carbon monoxide by the heater. Wasp nests in vent caps are common.
