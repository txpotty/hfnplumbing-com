    Why Choose HFN Plumbing for Grease Trap Cleaning in Durham, NC?

    Skipped pump-outs usually announce themselves at the floor drain first, with gray water pooling around the grate after the dish machine drains. The smell shows up next. That sour, rotten odor is grease breaking down and releasing hydrogen sulfide gas. The same gas corrodes concrete, which shortens the life of an in-ground interceptor.

    • Prevent Blockages, Regular pumping keeps floor drains flowing during the dinner rush.
    • Complete Pump-Outs, We pull grease, water and the settled solids every time.
    • Extended Lifespan, Less hydrogen sulfide in the tank means less corrosion on concrete and baffles.

    Citywide Coverage

    Hospitals and hotels cook around the clock, so there is no closed window. We work around that. The crew coordinates with your facilities manager, pumps during the slowest hour, and keeps the lid area roped off until the cover is back on and locked.

    Experienced Team

    Our techs have pumped enough traps to spot a cracked baffle by the way the grease cap sits. It shows in the details. An uneven cap tells them a baffle has come loose, and grease in the outlet chamber tells them the outlet tee is gone or plugged.

    Safety-First Approach

    We do not sell enzyme or bacteria additives as a replacement for pumping. Many programs do not allow it. Those products break grease into smaller droplets that pass through the outlet and re-form into hard deposits farther down the sewer, which is exactly the problem the trap exists to stop.

    Grease Trap Cleaning Cost in Durham, NC

    Cost follows trap size and fill level. Small under-sink traps go quickly.     Outdoor Grease Interceptor Pumping in Durham, NC

    In-ground interceptors need a vacuum truck and usually a pressure wand to break up the cap. Every compartment gets opened and pumped to the bottom. While the tank is empty, we look at the inlet and outlet tees, the baffle wall, the risers and the lid seals, because a cracked riser lets groundwater and dirt in. Lids go back on tight.

    Grease Trap Cleaning for Restaurants in Durham, NC

    Menu matters more than seat count. A sushi bar and a fried chicken place of the same size can need pumping on two completely different schedules. We ask what is on the line, how many fryers you run and how often the dish machine cycles. Then we measure.

    Residential Grease Trap Cleaning in Durham, NC

    Homes rarely have a grease trap, though a few large custom kitchens and some home-based catering setups in Durham, NC put a small unit under the sink. These are usually 10 to 20 gallon units. Service means scooping out grease and solids by hand, cleaning the baffle, and seating the gasket so the lid seals tight. About thirty minutes.

    Scheduled Grease Trap Maintenance in Durham

    Pumping frequency comes from two numbers: how big the trap is and how fast your kitchen fills it. We measure the second one with a sludge judge on the first few visits. After that the date goes on our calendar and the truck arrives without a reminder call from you. Schedules shift if your menu does.

    Preventive Maintenance Programs in Durham, NC

    A maintenance plan is just a pumping interval plus someone who tracks it. Your manager does not have to remember it, because we track the dates. If the sludge judge shows the trap hitting 20 percent between visits, we pull the next date in, and if it reads 10 percent, we push it out and save you a pump-out. Adjustments happen at every visit.

    Odor Control and Trap Inspections in Durham

    A trap that stinks between pump-outs usually has a flattened lid gasket or a vent problem. Both get checked every visit. A gasket costs little to replace, while a missing or broken vent can let sewer gas into the kitchen and also slow the trap's drainage, so that one is worth tracking down. Most smells get fixed that visit.

    Grease Trap Pumping and Hauling in Durham

    Trap waste is regulated as a liquid waste, so in most places it has to be hauled by a licensed hauler to an approved receiving facility, not dumped down a manhole or into a dumpster. We do both the pumping and the hauling. Our vacuum trucks pull the full contents of the trap into a sealed tank, and the load gets logged with gallons and destination. No barrels left behind.

    No permit is needed just to have a trap pumped. You do need to keep the records. Installing a new trap or interceptor, or changing the size of one, usually needs a plumbing permit and sometimes a pretreatment program review before the work starts.

    Under-sink traps take 30 to 60 minutes. Plan one to three hours for an outdoor interceptor. Big tanks with a hardened cap take the longest, since the cap has to be broken up with a pressure wand before the hose will pull it.

    Short term, slow drains and smell. Leave it long enough and the grease cap sets up like a brick. Hydrogen sulfide from the rotting grease also attacks concrete, so interceptors that go unpumped for years often need new baffles and risers well before their time.

    The terms get mixed up constantly. Most people use trap for the small indoor unit, usually under 100 gallons. Interceptor usually means the large tank in the ground outside, often 500 gallons and up, and codes size the two in different ways.
