    Gas Water Heater Installation in Winston-Salem, NC

    Gas heaters recover faster than electric, often refilling a 50 gallon tank in under an hour. Our crews set the gas line, the drip leg and the vent connector, then do a draft test at the hood to prove the exhaust is leaving the house. Then we check for leaks. We add an expansion tank if code calls for it.

    Commercial Water Heater Installation in Winston-Salem

    Restaurants, salons and offices burn through hot water at rates no household tank can keep up with. We install commercial tanks over 100,000 BTU and racked tankless systems, with the venting and gas supply those loads require. We schedule around your hours. Installs can happen after close.

    Expansion Tank Installation in Winston-Salem, NC

    A pressure reducing valve or check valve on the main turns your plumbing into a closed system. When the water heats and expands with nowhere to go, pressure climbs and stresses the relief valve, the toilet fill valves and every supply line in the house. An expansion tank absorbs it. We pre-charge it to match your water pressure.

    Attic and Closet Water Heater Installs in Winston-Salem

    Attic and closet installs take more planning than a garage swap. Every attic heater needs a drain pan with a line to the outside or a drain, a relief line routed properly and a clear path for the next tank to come in and out. Ceilings are at stake. Relocation to a safer spot is an option.

    Electric Water Heater Installation in Winston-Salem, NC

    Electric tanks need a 240-volt circuit, usually a 30-amp double-pole breaker with 10-gauge wire. Before hooking up the new tank we check that the breaker and wire size match the elements, since a mismatch is a common cause of tripped breakers and scorched terminals. They do not need a vent. That makes placement easier.

    Brand-Specific Water Heater Installation in Winston-Salem

    Each manufacturer publishes its own install rules, and they are not identical. Rheem, AO Smith, Bradford White and Navien each spell out clearances, vent lengths and relief valve placement, and a warranty claim can be denied if those were ignored. We follow them exactly. Your warranty stays intact.

    Traditional Tank Water Heater Installation in Winston-Salem

    Tank heaters still heat most homes' water, holding 40 to 75 gallons at temperature until you need it. We size the tank by first-hour rating, not just gallons, because that number tells you how much hot water it delivers in a busy morning. Either fuel works. We connect the fuel, set the vent and haul the old tank away.

    Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Winston-Salem, NC

    Water on the garage floor and no hot water usually means the tank has rusted through. Shut the cold supply valve on top of the tank and turn the gas knob to pilot or flip the breaker, then call us. We carry common sizes. Most emergency swaps finish the same day.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Installation in Winston-Salem

    Most installs in Winston-Salem, NC land between $1,200 and $3,500. Tank versus tankless, size, gas versus electric and any needed upgrades all move the number. Hard access costs more. We give you the figure before work starts.

    A tank heater usually lasts 8 to 12 years with maintenance. Tankless models tend to last 15 to 20 years.

    The job involves gas, venting, electrical and pressure safety. Licensed installs keep you safe and keep the warranty valid.

    A standard tank replacement usually takes two to four hours. Tankless or relocation work takes longer.

    Rusty hot water, rumbling from the tank, water at the base and an age past ten years are the big ones. Short hot water often points to heavy sediment or a broken dip tube. Any of those deserves a look.

    Rheem, Bradford White, A.O. Smith and Noritz are our most common installs. Both gas and electric models are available in a range of sizes, and special orders usually arrive fast. We help you choose.

    Start with correct sizing and a thermostat set at 120 degrees. Insulating the first few feet of hot pipe and flushing the tank once a year both help, and a tankless model cuts standby loss.

    An expansion tank absorbs the extra pressure created as water heats. Homes with a closed plumbing system are often required by code to have one, since it protects the heater and every pipe in the house.
