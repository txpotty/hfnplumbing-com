    Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining in Winston-Salem, NC

    CIPP stands for cured-in-place pipe. The crew saturates a felt tube with two-part epoxy on a roller table at the truck, invert it into the cleaned line with air pressure, and hold it against the old pipe wall until the resin hardens. Ambient cure takes a few hours, and hot water or steam shortens it. The finished liner seals cracks and leaking joints in the original pipe.

    Causes of Sewer Line Failure in Winston-Salem, NC

    Shifting soil is one of the main reasons sewer joints pull apart or crack. Coastal areas have sandy soil and high water tables, which affect how sewer lines and septic systems perform.     Pipe Bursting in Winston-Salem, NC

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. The new HDPE pipe is butt-fused into one length first.     Cast Iron Sewer Line Lining in Winston-Salem, NC

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. That is called channel rot. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, lining beats breaking up the floor in two rooms.

    Orangeburg and Clay Pipe Replacement in Winston-Salem

    Orangeburg is bituminous fiber pipe that was laid in houses mostly from the late 1940s into the early 1970s. Over time it softens, goes oval and blisters on the inside, so a liner has nothing round to bond to and bursting is usually the better fix. Clay is different. Clay that still holds its round shape is a good candidate for a liner.

    Pipe Bursting Sewer Line Replacement in Winston-Salem
